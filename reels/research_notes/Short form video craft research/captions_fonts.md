## 1. Which caption styles are in use, which niches is each tied to, and is there a 2025–2026 shift away from loud captions?

### Takeaway
Word-timed captions in a heavy sans with a white fill and a dark edge are the 2026 baseline; what varies is the loud layer (all caps, yellow or green active-word highlight, emoji, bounce, sound effects). Tool vendors call the full "Hormozi" package saturated and promote a quieter variant, but the shift is asserted, not measured: every source for it sells caption software, and no platform data, large-sample study or statement from the creators themselves was found.

### Cited Findings
**Style definitions, as the tools and vendors give them**
- **[CONSENSUS, vendors only]** "Hormozi style": heavy sans in all caps, thick black outline, word-by-word timing, about 3–5 words per line, the spoken word highlighted yellow or green while the rest stays white. No first-party confirmation from Hormozi or his editors. — [Kapwing template](https://www.kapwing.com/explore/hormozi-style-captions-template); [Fluxnote](https://fluxnote.io/guides/how-to-make-alex-hormozi-style-captions); [Blitzcut](https://blitzcutai.com/blog/best-caption-fonts-tiktok); [SendShort](https://sendshort.ai/guides/hormozi-captions/)
- **[VENDOR, conflicts with the line above]** Submagic says Hormozi's older look was The Bold Font, uppercase, with a shadow and no stroke, and that his newer look uses Anton. Most other vendors say Montserrat Black 900 with a thick outline. Unresolved. — [Submagic](https://www.submagic.co/blog/how-to-make-alex-hormozi-captions)
- **[VENDOR ×3]** "MrBeast / Beast style": comic display face, uppercase, heavy black stroke, pop animation; ordinary words black and white, keywords given a shadow. — [Submagic](https://www.submagic.co/blog/how-to-make-captions-like-mrbeast); [Filmora](https://filmora.wondershare.com/ai-generation/how-to-create-mrbeast-font.html); [OpenClip docs](https://openclip.app/docs/clipping/captions/)
- **[VENDOR]** "Karaoke": the phrase stays up and words light as they are spoken. "Mozi": Hormozi-inspired with a green highlight. "Clean" (OpenClip's default preset): white Inter, black stroke, soft shadow. OpenClip is a different product from OpusClip. — [OpenClip docs](https://openclip.app/docs/clipping/captions/)
- **[VENDOR ×3]** "Ali Abdaal style": white text with yellow highlights on key words. — [Choppity](https://www.choppity.com/tools/recreate-video-editing-style/ali-abdaal/); [Submagic](https://www.submagic.co/blog/make-captions-like-ali-abdaal); [SendShort](https://sendshort.ai/guides/ali-abdaal-captions/)
- **[VENDOR ×2]** "Iman Gadzhi style": Montserrat in light and bold; emphasis by switching weight, not colour. — [Submagic](https://submagic.co/blog/how-to-make-captions-like-iman-gadzhi); [SendShort](https://sendshort.ai/guides/captions-iman-gadzhi)
- **[VENDOR]** Platform-generated captions mostly use white text with a black outline or a solid background box. — [Kapwing](https://www.kapwing.com/resources/social-media-captions-styles-a-complete-guide/)
- **[VENDOR, no count given]** Plain white text with a black outline is described as the universal baseline, used in the majority of short-form content (paraphrase). — [Blitzcut](https://blitzcutai.com/blog/tiktok-caption-trends-2026)
- **[OFFICIAL tool docs / VENDOR]** Preset libraries shape the look: Captions lists 75+ templates; Submagic advertises 35+ animated styles, grouped as Trending, New, Emoji, Premium and Speakers, and names templates after Devin Jatho, Alex Hormozi, MrBeast, Iman Gadzhi and Ali Abdaal; OpusClip launched captions with 15+ built-in fonts. — [Captions help](https://captions.ai/help/web/captions/choosing-a-template); [Submagic](https://www.submagic.co/blog/make-captions-like-ali-abdaal); [Max Productive review](https://max-productive.ai/ai-tools/submagic/); [Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/p/opus-clip/opusclip-captions)

**Niche and audience**
- **[OFFICIAL tool docs, guidance without data]** Captions (the app): a style that performs on TikTok can feel out of place on LinkedIn; clean, minimal styles for professional content; for entertainment and TikTok-style content it suggests script-adjacent or refined sans presets (its "Sonnet" and "Analog"). — [Captions: styles by platform](https://captions.ai/help/guides/engagement/caption-styles-by-platform); [Captions: choosing a style](https://captions.ai/help/guides/engagement/choosing-a-style)
- **[VENDOR]** ClipSpeedAI positions its Hormozi preset as bold, clean text with strong contrast and minimal animation, designed for authority and impact, and offers minimal styles for business and educational content. — [ClipSpeedAI](https://www.clipspeed.ai/blog/best-captions-app-for-short-videos.html)
- **[VENDOR, disagrees with the two lines above]** Blitzcut: minimal styles suit accessibility, long-form YouTube and dense technical content, but as the main short-form format they signal inattention to production quality (paraphrase). — [Blitzcut](https://blitzcutai.com/blog/tiktok-caption-trends-2026)

**Evidence on a shift**
- **[VENDOR opinion, no data]** Blitzcut's 2026 trend piece: Hormozi style is saturated ("the full yellow-highlight package no longer differentiates"); word-by-word captions are the settled baseline; "Dynamic Minimalism" is the new meta; TikTok Sans is rising. — [Blitzcut](https://blitzcutai.com/blog/tiktok-caption-trends-2026)
- **[VENDOR]** Joyspace (2026): the current approach "keeps the mechanics of Hormozi (word-by-word captions, cuts, zooms) but strips away the noise (emojis, flashing colors, sound effects)". — [Joyspace](https://joyspace.ai/hormozi-editing-style-2026-analysis)
- **[VENDOR, no dataset]** Ascynd: the relative advantage of Hormozi-style captions is smaller in 2026 than in 2023. — [Ascynd](https://ascynd.io/en/blog/why-hormozi-captions-get-more-views)
- **[VENDOR self-test, n=80 videos, no method published]** FluxNote tested 7 animated caption styles against plain auto-captions; word-by-word emphasis with colour did best, +34% watch time. This points against "plain is winning". — [FluxNote](https://fluxnote.io/blog/ai-captions-that-improve-watch-time)
- **[VENDOR, no design disclosed]** ClipForge says it analysed 50,000+ clips and found 12–28% variance from caption style. — [ClipForge](https://clip-forge.io/blog/ai-caption-styles-engagement-data-2026)
- **[OFFICIAL]** Platform-native styling is growing: Instagram's Edits app generates captions "with one button press", has a bulk editor and a style editor, and Instagram says it is "regularly releasing new fonts, animations, and effects". A third-party guide counts about 50 fonts grouped Simple, Bold, Handwritten (attribution uncertain: Buffer or Later). — [Instagram Creators blog](https://creators.instagram.com/blog/discover-edits-tools-built-with-creators-to-help-you-stand-out/); [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/how-to-use-instagram-edits/)
- **[Negative finding]** A targeted search for any statement that Alex Hormozi dropped the style, or found that less-edited videos perform better, returned no such statement, only vendor pages such as this one. — [Joyspace](https://joyspace.ai/hormozi-editing-style-2026-analysis)

### Inferences
- The stable core (word-level timing, heavy sans, white fill, dark edge) can be separated from the fashion layer (caps, yellow or green highlight, emoji, bounce, sound effects). Caption rules can fix the core and make the loud layer an optional preset.
- For educational and business talking-head content the vendor consensus points to a quiet variant: short word-timed chunks, one accent colour or a weight change, no emoji or flashing colours. This is a consensus of sellers, not a measured result.
- Vendors profit from calling last year's preset saturated. Their own tests still show highlighted, animated captions beating plain auto-captions, so the supported reading is "word-level emphasis still helps; how loud it should be is unresolved".

### Gaps
- No count of what top accounts use (for example caption style across the top 100 business and education creators per platform). It needs manual sampling of videos, which this session could not do.
- No first-party statement from Hormozi, MrBeast, Ali Abdaal, Iman Gadzhi or their editors. Every attribution comes from vendors selling look-alike presets.
- OpusClip's "11 Editing Aesthetics Dominating Short-Form in 2026" and "Best Caption Presets & Styles that Boost Retention" surfaced but their content was not extracted. — [OpusClip](https://www.opus.pro/blog/editing-aesthetics-dominating-short-form-2026)
- No independent data on whether loud captions help or hurt by niche.

## 2. Exact fonts and weights documented for named creators and the dominant caption tools, with licence status

### Takeaway
Montserrat (Black or ExtraBold), Inter and TikTok Sans are the well-documented caption fonts confirmed here as open (SIL OFL 1.1), so free to use commercially and to bundle. The fonts vendors tie to specific creators are paid or unclear: TT Fors is paid (its free trial is evaluation-only), Komika Axis and The Bold Font have conflicting licence listings, and Instagram Sans is proprietary.

### Inferences
- Weight range in documented practice is 700–900. The 900 end belongs to the all-caps Hormozi look; clean presets sit at 700–800.
- A pipeline that must ship fonts with its code can cover the documented looks with OFL families alone: Montserrat Black for the Hormozi look, Inter for the clean look, TikTok Sans for a platform-native feel. The creator-specific fonts (TT Fors, Komika Axis, The Bold Font, CCSignLanguage) add licence cost or risk for little visible difference.
- Vendor "what font does X use" pages are identification by eye and sometimes contradict each other (Hormozi: Montserrat vs The Bold Font vs Anton; MrBeast: Komika Axis vs CCSignLanguage). Treat them as "looks like", not as documentation.

### Gaps
- Not verified in this session, **[RECALL]**: Anton, Poppins, Bebas Neue and Open Sans are published on Google Fonts under open licences (OFL). Confirm at [Google Fonts](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Anton).
- Not verified, **[RECALL]**: Proxima Nova (Mark Simonson Studio) and Helvetica Now (Monotype) are paid commercial families; SF Pro is Apple's system font, whose licence restricts it to Apple-platform interface work; CCSignLanguage is a paid Comicraft font. No source in this session documents a named top creator using Proxima Nova, Helvetica Now, SF Pro or Bebas Neue for captions.
- Default fonts of CapCut, Submagic and Captions presets are not published in anything the search returned; they would have to be read off the apps.
- The designer's own licence line for The Bold Font and the Apostrophic Labs licence text for Komika Axis were not seen.
- Which fonts Instagram's caption sticker, TikTok's auto-captions and the Shorts text tool use was not researched (search budget).

## 3. Numbers: size, words per caption, characters per line, case, edge treatment, highlight colours, emoji, entrance animation, sync

### Takeaway
Vendor consensus for a 1080×1920 frame: 60–75 px bold or black sans (70–85 px for karaoke styles), white fill, 3–4 px black stroke with an optional soft shadow, 3–5 words per caption (2–3 for faster pacing), yellow or green active-word highlight, and a pop-in of roughly 100–150 ms peaking at 115–130%. Every number comes from tool blogs and editing tutorials, none from a platform or a controlled test, and sources disagree on line length (3–5 words vs 5–7) and on stroke versus shadow.

### Cited Findings
**Size**
- **[VENDOR, three pages of one seller]** 60–75 px for TikTok, Reels and Shorts alike ("all three platforms use the same 9:16 vertical frame"); karaoke-style 70–85 px. — [Blitzcut TikTok](https://blitzcutai.com/blog/best-caption-size-tiktok-2026); [Blitzcut Reels](https://blitzcutai.com/blog/best-caption-size-instagram-reels-2026); [Blitzcut Shorts](https://blitzcutai.com/blog/best-caption-size-youtube-shorts-2026)
- **[SINGLE ×2, attribution uncertain]** Two other guides in the same results give 48–70 px (warning that above 72 px crowds the safe zone) and 60–80 px, described as about 7–9% of frame height. Candidates: Rev, OpenClip. — [Rev](https://www.rev.com/blog/best-open-caption-font-sizes-for-videos-on-social-media); [OpenClip](https://openclip.app/guides/caption-styling-guide)
- **[VENDOR relaying a platform; primary not seen]** Facebook guidance sets a floor of at least 48 px on 1080×1920. — [OpusClip Facebook Reels guide](https://www.opus.pro/blog/facebook-reels-caption-subtitle-best-practices)
- **[VENDOR, unit unclear]** OpusClip's Shorts guide says 18–24 "points". Points do not map onto the pixel figures above. — [OpusClip](https://www.opus.pro/blog/youtube-shorts-caption-subtitle-best-practices)
- **[SINGLE]** Premiere Pro setting for the Hormozi look: Montserrat Black, size 85 (sequence size not stated). — [Vidpros](https://vidpros.com/how-to-edit-videos-like-alex-hormozi/)
- **[OFFICIAL, content not extracted]** CapCut uses its own font units and publishes a conversion to pixels. — [CapCut help](https://www.capcut.com/help/font-units-in-capcut)

**Words and characters**
- **[CONSENSUS, vendors]** Hormozi look: about 3–5 words per line. — [Fluxnote](https://fluxnote.io/guides/how-to-make-alex-hormozi-style-captions)
- **[VENDOR]** 3–5 words per caption segment for short-form; 2–3 words "feel faster and more energetic"; a sister guide says 2–5. — [OpenClip](https://openclip.app/guides/caption-styling-guide)
- **[VENDOR, conflicts]** OpusClip: 32–42 characters per line, roughly five to seven words, for Shorts; elsewhere 42 characters maximum per line; elsewhere 32 for one line and 64 across two. — [OpusClip Shorts](https://www.opus.pro/blog/youtube-shorts-caption-subtitle-best-practices); [OpusClip design](https://www.opus.pro/blog/video-caption-design-placement); [OpusClip readability](https://www.opus.pro/blog/video-captions-for-maximum-readability)
- **[Negative finding]** A targeted search found no rigorous study comparing 1–3-word captions with full-sentence captions on retention; vendor guides recommend 1–3 words at a time without data (attribution among them uncertain). — [Kompozy](https://kompozy.io/how-to/video-captions-for-retention); [ClipSpeedAI on DEV](https://dev.to/kyle_clipspeedai/why-captions-are-the-most-underrated-growth-lever-in-short-form-video-3ogm)

**Case, edge treatment, colour**
- **[CONSENSUS, vendors]** All caps for the Hormozi and MrBeast looks. — [Kapwing](https://www.kapwing.com/explore/hormozi-style-captions-template); [Submagic](https://www.submagic.co/blog/how-to-make-captions-like-mrbeast)
- **[VENDOR]** Stroke: 3–4 px black on white text, plus an optional soft drop shadow. — [Blitzcut](https://blitzcutai.com/blog/how-to-make-captions-look-professional)
- **[VENDOR, conflict]** Stroke versus shadow: Submagic describes Hormozi's older look as shadow with no stroke; other vendors specify a thick black outline; MrBeast look is a "high" black stroke. — [Submagic Hormozi](https://www.submagic.co/blog/how-to-make-alex-hormozi-captions); [Submagic MrBeast](https://www.submagic.co/blog/how-to-make-captions-like-mrbeast)
- **[VENDOR ×3, values differ]** Highlight yellow: #F7C204 (Blitzcut), #FDE047 (Fluxnote), #EAC70C (Kapwing). Highlight green: #02FB23 (one source, attribution uncertain). — [Blitzcut](https://blitzcutai.com/hormozi-caption-app); [Fluxnote](https://fluxnote.io/guides/how-to-make-alex-hormozi-style-captions); [Kapwing](https://www.kapwing.com/explore/hormozi-style-captions-template)
- **[VENDOR]** Emphasis without colour: Iman Gadzhi look switches weight (light to bold). — [Submagic](https://submagic.co/blog/how-to-make-captions-like-iman-gadzhi)

**Emoji**
- **[SINGLE]** Submagic keeps an "Emoji" template category. **[VENDOR]** Joyspace lists emoji among the "noise" the 2026 approach removes. No performance data either way. — [Max Productive](https://max-productive.ai/ai-tools/submagic/); [Joyspace](https://joyspace.ai/hormozi-editing-style-2026-analysis)

**Entrance animation**
- **[SINGLE tutorial, attribution uncertain, likely FlexClip]** Pop: scale 0% → 130% in 4 frames → 100% in 4 more frames. — [FlexClip](https://www.flexclip.com/learn/how-to-animate-captions-in-premiere-pro.html)
- **[SINGLE forum answer]** After Effects overshoot: 0% → about 133% → 100%, offset by "a few frames". — [Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/t5/after-effects-discussions/expression-to-scale-up-text-from-0-with-overshoot/m-p/10522753/highlight/true)
- **[SINGLE, unofficial community preset]** "TikTok" pop: grow 100–150 ms, shrink 100–150 ms, peak 115–120%, labelled "fast, punchy". — [ffmpeg kinetic captions skill](https://claudskills.com/skills/ffmpeg-kinetic-captions/SKILL.md)
- **[SINGLE forum answer]** Premiere's caption track is not built for per-word scale keyframes; captions must be split or converted to graphics. — [Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/t5/premiere-pro/captions-scale-size-editing/m-p/11843934)
- A ready-made "Popping Word Captions" component exists for Remotion (parameters not extracted). — [Remotion](https://www.remotion.dev/elements/captions/popping-word-captions.md)

### Inferences

- So at 70–85 px one line holds about 14–26 characters, roughly 3–4 words. This is why practice converges on 3–5 words per caption in one or two lines. Cap height is 0.70× the font size in Montserrat and 0.73× in Inter. All caps costs 15–18% of line capacity against sentence case in the same font.
- The 32–42 characters per line that OpusClip repeats only fits an 840 px line at about 31–40 px (all-caps Montserrat Black) or 41–53 px (sentence-case Inter Bold). That is below the 60–85 px range the same class of sources recommends, so those two pieces of guidance cannot both be followed.
- **Animation in frames.** 100–150 ms is 3–4.5 frames at 30 fps (6–9 at 60 fps). The 4+4-frame tutorial pop is 267 ms at 30 fps. At 180 words per minute a word lasts 333 ms on average, so a 267 ms pop uses 80% of a single word's time while a 100–150 ms pop uses 30–45%. For one-word-at-a-time captions the entrance should stay at or under about 4 frames at 30 fps. (The 180 wpm rate is an assumption.)

## 5. Evidence that captions change performance, and whether the common statistics hold up

### Takeaway
No platform-published or peer-reviewed measurement of caption lift on Reels, TikTok or Shorts was found. The best-documented numbers are old and not about short-form: Facebook's 2016 internal test (+12% view time for captioned video ads) and the 2019 Verizon Media and Publicis Media survey (5,616 US adults; 69% watch sound-off in public; 80% say they are more likely to finish a captioned video, which is stated intent, not behaviour). ## 6. Readability and accessibility guidance: contrast, reading speed, line length, WCAG-style rules for burned-in captions

### Takeaway
This question was only partly researched: the searches for the formal standards (WCAG, Netflix, BBC, DCMP) were refused when the budget ran out. What was collected shows caption-tool blogs repeating broadcast norms (32–42 characters per line, 1.5–6 seconds on screen) that do not fit 60–85 px type on a 1080 px frame, and the contrast arithmetic shows why a stroke, box or shade is needed: unboxed white text fails 4.5:1 on any background lighter than mid-grey.

### Cited Findings
- **[VENDOR]** Maximum 42 characters per line, 1–6 seconds on screen, captions within the middle 80% of the width. — [OpusClip caption design](https://www.opus.pro/blog/video-caption-design-placement)
- **[VENDOR, same seller, different numbers]** Maximum 32 characters for a single line and 64 across two lines; minimum display time 1.5 seconds. — [OpusClip readability](https://www.opus.pro/blog/video-captions-for-maximum-readability)
- **[VENDOR]** Bold sans-serif fonts "consistently outperform decorative or serif fonts on small screens"; avoid thin or light weights. No data shown. — [OpenClip](https://openclip.app/guides/caption-styling-guide)
- **[VENDOR]** A high-contrast outline, background or active-text highlight "will ensure better message clarity". — [Kapwing](https://www.kapwing.com/resources/social-media-captions-styles-a-complete-guide/)
- **[VENDOR relaying a platform]** Text at least 48 px on 1080×1920. — [OpusClip Facebook Reels guide](https://www.opus.pro/blog/facebook-reels-caption-subtitle-best-practices)
- **[ACADEMIC paper; authors, year and sample not captured in the extract]** A paper on caption width for deaf and hard-of-hearing viewers cites about 6 words per line as the existing guideline and argues it may be too narrow; it also notes that one-word-at-a-time presentation allows much faster reading than conventional reading. It measures comfort and preference, not retention. — [NSF Public Access Repository](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10253921)
- **[STUDY, 2019 survey, n=5,616]** 80% of people who use captions are not hearing-impaired, so caption readability is a mainstream concern as well as an accessibility one. — [Streaming Media](https://www.streamingmedia.com/Articles/News/Online-Video-News/80-of-Video-Caption-Users-Arent-Hearing-Impaired-Finds-Verizon-131860.aspx)

### Inferences
- **[LOCAL calculation, WCAG 2.x contrast formula]** Contrast ratios for the colours vendors report:

| Text colour | On black | Against white (neighbouring words) |
|---|---|---|
| White #FFFFFF | 21.0:1 | n/a |
| Yellow #F7C204 (Blitzcut) | 12.7:1 | 1.66:1 |
| Yellow #FDE047 (Fluxnote) | 15.9:1 | 1.32:1 |
| Yellow #EAC70C (Kapwing) | 12.7:1 | 1.66:1 |
| Green #02FB23 | 14.8:1 | 1.42:1 |

- White text reaches 4.5:1 only on backgrounds at or below a relative luminance of 0.183 (grey #767676 or darker) and 3:1 only at or below 0.300 (about #949494 or darker). Light skin, sky, white walls and light clothing are usually brighter than that, so white captions need a black stroke, a box or a darkened band behind them.
- Yellow and green highlights differ from white neighbours by hue, not by luminance (1.3–1.7:1). In greyscale or for some colour-vision deficiencies the highlighted word barely stands out. If the highlight carries meaning, pair it with a second cue such as scale, weight or a box.
- **Reading speed arithmetic** (5.54 characters per word including spaces, from the measurement sample): 150 words per minute is 13.9 characters per second, 180 is 16.6, 210 is 19.4, 220 is 20.3. Verbatim captions exceed a 20 characters-per-second ceiling (the Netflix figure recalled under Gaps) only when the speaker passes about 217 words per minute.
- **Display scale changes what counts as large text.** A 1080 px frame shown on a phone viewport about 390 CSS px wide (an assumption) is scaled by 0.36, so 48 px in the video displays at about 17 CSS px, 60 px at about 22 and 85 px at about 31. Against the large-text thresholds recalled under Gaps (18.7 CSS px bold, 24 regular), bold captions qualify as large text from about 52 px in the video frame and regular-weight captions only from about 67 px.
- **Word-by-word display breaks broadcast minimum durations by design.** At 180 words per minute a single word is on screen for about 0.33 s and a 3-word chunk for about 1.0 s, against the 1.5 s minimum OpusClip repeats. The academic note above (single-word presentation supports fast reading) is the only collected support for why this still reads; no accessibility source was collected on whether it serves deaf and hard-of-hearing viewers.
- **[LOCAL context]** The pipeline's existing internal rules (contrast target 4.5:1, or 3:1 for large text; comfortable reading up to 17 characters per second and unreadable above 25; blocks of at most two lines) are consistent with the figures recalled under Gaps, but they carry no source in the skill file.

## 7. On-screen hook titles: size, position, length, and how they differ from running captions

### Takeaway
Almost nothing verifiable was collected: the search budget ran out before this question was researched. The gathered sources support only this much: put the hook above the captions and below the top interface band (270–290 px from the top clears the Meta and Google ad templates), and treat it as a separate, larger element. Sizes, word counts and durations remain unsourced.

### Cited Findings
- **[VENDOR]** "Put hooks higher, captions in the lower-middle, and CTAs closer to the center or middle-left." — [Reap](https://reap.video/blog/short-form-video-safe-zones)
- **[OFFICIAL→3P]** Top bands a hook title must clear in ads: 269 px (Meta, 14%), 288 px (Google vertical-ad template), 240 px (TikTok In-Feed template, third-party reading). — [Terrads](https://terrads.com/blog/meta-safe-zones-are-percentages); [Socialync YouTube](https://www.socialync.io/tools/safe-zones/youtube-shorts); [Socialync TikTok](https://www.socialync.io/tools/safe-zones/tiktok)
- **[VENDOR estimates]** Top bands in organic posts: 108 px (TikTok), 120 px (Shorts), 210–270 px (Reels). — [Adaptlypost](https://adaptlypost.com/blog/social-media-safe-zones-2026-complete-guide); [Revid](https://www.revid.ai/instagram-reels-safe-zone)
- **[SINGLE, a retailer's brand system]** Type-safe area starts 260 px from the top of a 1080×1920 frame. — [Canadian Tire design system](https://brand-qa2.canadiantire.ca/en/ctr/design-system/omni-channel/motion/margins-and-safe-space.html)
- **[OFFICIAL→3P, about 2021–2022, primary not found]** TikTok videos with on-screen text or captions got 55.7% more impressions than videos with none; one retelling ties this to text "that displays a call to action". This is the closest collected evidence that added on-screen text, hook titles included, pays off. — [Hypebot](https://www.hypebot.com/how-to-get-your-tiktok-to-go-viral/)
- **[SINGLE blog, unverified attribution to TikTok for Business]** Ads using text overlays see 74% higher attention and 3.3× more action than non-native formats. — [Coinis](https://coinis.com/how-to/best-way-to-text-overlay-tiktok-ad)

### Inferences
- A hook title that must be safe on all three platforms' ad templates starts at or below y=288 and stays within x 120–888. For organic-only posts it can start around y=210–270 (the Reels estimate is the binding one).
- The hook and the captions compete for the same reader, so they should not be on screen together in different zones; the collected sources place hooks high and captions low-middle but none addresses timing between them.
- **[LOCAL context]** The pipeline's own convention is a hook of about 92 px bold against captions of 56–66 px, a ratio of 1.4–1.6, with subtitles hidden while a hook headline is on screen. No external source collected here confirms or contradicts those numbers.
