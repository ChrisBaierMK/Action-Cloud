## 1. Cut frequency: how often top videos cut, how pacing differs by niche and platform, and whether editing slowed down in 2024–2026

### Takeaway
No large-sample or frame-level measurement of shot length in top-performing talking-head shorts turned up; the usable numbers are practitioner heuristics that cluster at one cut or visual change every 2–5 seconds (extremes: 1.5 seconds and 8–10 seconds), plus one official TikTok ad study that rewards five or more scenes. The turn away from hyper-fast "retention editing" is documented as creator statements and commentary about long-form YouTube, not as short-form performance data.

### Cited Findings

#### Official and measured data
- **[Official: TikTok ad research, paid conversion ads]** Ads with five or more distinct scenes showed a 171% conversion lift over ads with fewer than five. Multi-scene ads showed a 38% lift over videos of one person selling a product in one continuous shot. TikTok says the findings were validated globally except in Brazil, Japan and North America, and that awareness-focused advertisers should follow impression-focused practices instead. — [TikTok for Business blog, "Triple-digit conversion lift"](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-US/blog/creative-that-drives-conversions)
- **[Official: TikTok guidance, page not pinned]** A TikTok for Business guide recommends 21–34 seconds for most ads, with quick cuts and varied angles, and advises against static talking heads. — one of [Creative best practices for performance ads](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/creative-best-practices), [TikTok creative strategy guide for small businesses](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en/blog/tiktok-creative-strategy-small-business), [SMB Creative Playbook (PDF)](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/library/SMB_Creative_Playbook.pdf)
- **[Official: TikTok Creative Codes]** The "Stimulation" code reads: "Powerful attention triggers like music, dynamic transitions, movement, text overlays, and emojis keep viewers interested." The supporting research is old: a Kantar analysis of 3,500 TikTok ads that ran from 1 January to 1 October 2021. — [TikTok Creative Codes](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-AU/creative-codes)
- **[Academic: preprint, June 2026, about 10,000 videos]** A study of TikTok videos from Estonian brand and organisational accounts coded "editing pace" and found it significantly associated with likes, comments, shares and saves. "Jump cuts prominent" showed weak or no significance. The content features gave "modest but consistent gains" over account-size and video-age baselines. The summary did not show the direction or units of the editing-pace effect. — [arXiv 2606.16053](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.16053)
- **[Academic: working paper, 2026, seven-condition experiment]** Dost and Huang, "Jump Cut Editing Style and Transition Frequency Differentially Affect Interactive and Sustained Engagement in Short-Form Video". Their hypotheses: seamless jump cuts earn more likes than overlapping jump cuts or unedited video; overlapping jump cuts earn more continued watching and rewatching; higher transition frequency strengthens the watching effect up to a maximum. One search summary reported "seamless cuts increase liking" as a result; another showed only hypotheses. Results unconfirmed. — [Marketing Trends Congress 2026 paper](https://archives.marketing-trends-congress.com/2026/pages/PDF/paper_professor_DOST_HUANG.pdf)
- **[Academic: bachelor's thesis, 2026]** "The impact of video duration and editing pace on attention and engagement in TikTok" (Cristella Lankots, University of Tartu) exists; only its catalogue record was retrievable, not its findings. — [University of Tartu DSpace](https://dspace.ut.ee/items/cb8dc46c-0841-4fed-91af-17db99f1b2d4/full)
- **[Study: vendor, 13.5 million clips, January–March 2026]** OpusClip's analysis of clips processed by its own tool reports no cut-frequency or shot-length metric. It calls pacing "the biggest lever you have after captions" and recommends varying shot length. Sub-analyses used 34,635 clips (hook types), 14,213 (storylines) and 10,598 (tone); paid-promoted clips were excluded; the company calls the findings directional. — [OpusClip, "50 Video Editing Tips"](https://www.opus.pro/blog/video-editing-tips); [OpusClip, "The Anatomy of a Viral TikTok in 2026"](https://www.opus.pro/blog/anatomy-of-a-viral-tiktok-2026)
- **[Reference: film studies, page not pinned]** Average shot length in contemporary films is about 4–6 seconds, down from 8–11 seconds in classical Hollywood. — probably [Wikipedia, "Post-classical editing"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-classical_editing)

#### Platform differences
- **[Study: creator-analyst, April 2023; 33 channels, 5,400 Shorts, 3.3 billion views]** Shorts with under 60% "viewed vs swiped away" rarely performed well; secondary write-ups put the good range at 70–90%. The advice attached is to make the first second punchy. — [Paddy Galloway thread](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1646898356419981315.html); [Digital Information World](https://www.digitalinformationworld.com/2023/04/paddy-galloway-decoded-youtube-shorts.html)
- **[Practitioner: vendor; page not pinned]** Instagram is said to favour clean text, cohesive colour and on-brand styling, while the "TikTok edit" has split into many aesthetics. — probably [OpusClip, "11 Editing Aesthetics"](https://www.opus.pro/blog/editing-aesthetics-dominating-short-form-2026)

### Inferences
- Converted to the requested unit: one change every 2–5 seconds is 2–5 cuts or visual changes per 10 seconds. The aggressive end (1.5–3 seconds) is 3.3–6.7 per 10 seconds; the relaxed end (3–6 seconds) is 1.7–3.3 per 10 seconds.
- TikTok's five-scene finding inside its own 21–34 second recommendation implies an average scene of roughly 4–7 seconds or shorter. That is the only cadence with platform data behind it, and the data concerns paid conversion ads.
- The two camps mostly talk about different things. One counts cuts in the speech track (dead-time removal); the other counts picture changes. A rule consistent with both: never leave dead air, and change the picture every 2–5 seconds unless something is visibly developing in the frame, in which case holds of 8–10 seconds are defensible.
- The "slowdown" evidence supports less decoration and more story in long-form video. It does not show that slower cutting wins in shorts, and no 2025–2026 measurement either way was found.
- The academic signal (editing pace matters, prominent jump cuts do not) suggests jump cuts are neither punished nor rewarded in themselves; pace is what carries the association.

### Gaps
- No frame-level or large-sample shot-length dataset for top-performing talking-head or educational shorts was found. Tools that claim to measure average shot length and cuts per minute exist (for example [an "AI Video Breakdown" tool on Apify](https://apify.com/memo23/ai-video-breakdown)), but their accuracy is unverified and no published dataset from them surfaced.
- No source compared cut rates by niche or by platform with data.
- Searches refused when the budget ran out: Google's "ABCDs" creative guidance for Shorts, Meta's Reels creative guidance, and details of the vidIQ argument.
- Unverified lead (background knowledge, not checked this session): a MrBeast production onboarding document leaked in 2024 discusses retention by video segment; it concerns long-form.

## 2. Silence and pause removal, jump cuts and punch-in zooms

### Takeaway
Tool documentation and editor guides converge on a workable starting preset: treat audio below roughly −30 to −40 dB as silence, cut gaps longer than about 0.25–0.5 seconds and keep 0.05–0.25 seconds of padding on each side of a cut, which typically shortens a talking-head take by 15–25%. Jump cuts are hidden with punch-ins of about 8–15% (110–115% scale), while slower push-ins run to 115–125%; nothing found supports 130%, and none of these numbers is tied to retention data.

### Cited Findings

#### Detection thresholds and padding
- **[Practitioner: Adobe community forum]** Any silence remover needs two settings: the level that counts as silence, and the minimum length of silence to remove. — [Adobe community thread](https://community.adobe.com/t5/premiere-pro-ideas/automatically-remove-silence-from-a-video/idi-p/13577633)
- **[Tool documentation]** auto-editor keeps a default margin of 0.2 seconds around loud sections ("0.2s" is the documented default for `--margin`). — [auto-editor README](https://github.com/wyattblue/auto-editor)
- **[Tool documentation, conflict]** auto-editor's default loudness threshold appears as 4% (`audio:threshold=0.04`) in older releases, while the current README uses decibel syntax. The current default could not be confirmed. — [PyPI, auto-editor 24.29.1](https://pypi.org/project/auto-editor/24.29.1)
- **[Tool documentation]** TimeBolt's manual gives example padding of about 0.10 seconds on the left and 0.15 seconds on the right, and a much smaller 0.01-second left pad for unscripted video with sharp speech starts. — [TimeBolt QuickStart Manual (PDF)](https://timebolt-assets.s3.us-east-1.amazonaws.com/TimeBolt_QuickStart_Manual.pdf)
- **[Third-party guide: page not pinned]** TimeBolt sets its threshold as a duration, in a "FastForward silences longer than" field, in seconds. — probably [TechPP](https://techpp.com/?p=185937)
- **[Single claim: page not pinned]** Recommended preset: −30 to −35 dB, a 0.25–0.4 second minimum gap, and about 0.05–0.1 seconds of padding on each side; more aggressive settings clip word endings. — one of [Cutsio](https://cutsio.com/blog/final-cut-pro-remove-silence-best-settings-for-natural-cuts), [Root Nation](https://root-nation.com/en/soft-en/lifehacks/en-silence-detection-settings-explained-volume-threshold-minimum-duration-and-buffer/), [BlitzCut](https://blitzcutai.com/blog/how-to-remove-silence-youtube-shorts)
- **[Single claim, disagrees with the previous one: page not pinned]** Recommended preset for natural-sounding dialogue: −35 to −40 dB with 0.3–0.5 seconds of padding. — same three candidate pages, for example [Root Nation](https://root-nation.com/en/soft-en/lifehacks/en-silence-detection-settings-explained-volume-threshold-minimum-duration-and-buffer/)
- **[Tool default: page not pinned]** One silence-cutting tool lists −30 dB threshold, 0.5-second minimum silence and 0.15-second padding as its values. — probably [this browser tool's page](https://pdfbolt.pages.dev/video/auto-cut-silence)
- **[Tool documentation]** FireCut suggests 250 ms of padding at the start and 250 ms at the end as a starting point. — [FireCut, Remove silences](https://learn.firecut.ai/features/remove-silences)
- **[Tool documentation]** AutoCut exposes four controls: noise level in dB ("silences are all segments below this threshold"), "remove silences over" (minimum silence length in seconds), "remove noises under" (minimum length of a sound to keep), and separate padding before and after each cut. Its default values were not shown. — [AutoCut Silences settings](https://autocut.com/en/help-center/autocut-silences/settings)
- **[Tool documentation and user reports]** Descript's "Shorten word gaps" has a user-set threshold; no default was shown. One user searches for gaps above 0.3 seconds; another shortens gaps to 0.05 seconds. — [Descript help](https://help.descript.com/script-editing/shorten-word-gaps); [Descript feature request](https://feedback.descript.com/feature-requests/p/shorten-word-gaps-with-an-upper-bound-and-or-time-multiplier)
- **[Tool limitation]** Descript users have asked for separate handling of gaps in the middle of a sentence and gaps between sentences, which implies the tool treats them alike. — [Descript feature request](https://feedback.descript.com/feature-requests/p/in-shorten-word-gaps-feature-enable-distinction-between-gaps-in-the-middle-of-se)

#### How much is removed, and what goes wrong
- **[Single claim: page not pinned]** Silence removal cuts 15–25% of total duration on a typical talking-head recording. — one of [OpusClip, AI silence removers](https://www.opus.pro/blog/best-ai-silence-removers), [Wideframe](https://try-wideframe.pages.dev/blog/how-to-edit-talking-head-videos-faster-with-ai), [Vizard](https://agent.vizard.ai/how-to/cut-the-pauses-and-stutters-out-of-a-raw-talking-head-video.html)
- **[Single claim: worked example]** "A 64-second talking head with natural breathing pauses came down to 48 seconds with no lines removed" (25% shorter). — [Jupitrr](https://jupitrr.com/how-to/edit-talking-head-videos)
- **[Practitioner: page not pinned]** Cutting too tight "sounds breathless, the speaker's head jumps at every cut, and music or room tone behind the speech stutters at the joins". — one of [Nemo Video](https://www.nemovideo.com/blog/ai-video-editing/remove-silence-from-audio), [Vizard](https://agent.vizard.ai/how-to/cut-the-pauses-and-stutters-out-of-a-raw-talking-head-video.html)
- **[Practitioner: page not pinned]** Level thresholds misfire: quiet background music can be read as silence, and a fan running under the voice can hide a pause. — same candidate pages, for example [Nemo Video](https://www.nemovideo.com/blog/ai-video-editing/remove-silence-from-audio)

#### Hiding jump cuts with punch-ins
- **[Practitioner: page not pinned]** "Add a slight zoom (10 to 15 percent) on one side of the cut." An instant cut to a tighter crop covers the seam so the jump reads as intentional. — one of [Jupitrr](https://jupitrr.com/how-to/edit-talking-head-videos), [OpusClip, auto jump-cut editors](https://www.opus.pro/blog/auto-jump-cut-editors)
- **[Practitioner: vendor tutorial]** Cut the clip, select the middle segment and raise its scale to about 110–115% so the cut reads as a zoom; add ease-in and ease-out keyframes when the zoom is animated. — [FireCut, Premiere Pro guide](https://firecut.ai/blog/how-to-zoom-in-on-premiere-pro-a-step-by-step-guide-for-beginners/)
- **[Practitioner: vendor tutorial]** Quick punch-ins of roughly 8–15% that land within about 12 frames suit short-form; slower push-ins go from 100% to about 115–125% over several seconds. — [Pippit](https://www.pippit.ai/blog/capcut-alternative/capcut-zoom-in-effect)
- **[Practitioner: tutorial transcript]** Other fixes: crop from 4K wide footage, place B-roll just before the cut, and add audio fades. — [Sozai transcript, "Hide jump cuts"](https://sozai.app/transcript/hide-jump-cuts-pro/)
- **[Academic]** In about 10,000 brand TikToks, prominent jump cuts showed weak or no association with engagement. — [arXiv 2606.16053](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.16053)

### Inferences
- A defensible starting preset from the overlap of the sources: silence threshold −30 to −40 dB (tune per recording, because room noise moves it), minimum silence 0.25–0.5 seconds, padding 0.05–0.25 seconds per side. That leaves roughly 0.1–0.5 seconds of audible pause at each cut.
- The padding disagreement (0.05–0.1 seconds against 0.3–0.5 seconds) maps onto style: tight padding gives the clipped social look, wide padding sounds like natural speech. Neither side cites retention data.
- Twelve frames is 0.4 seconds at 30 fps and 0.2 seconds at 60 fps, so the quoted "fast" punch-in is a 0.2–0.4 second move, or an instant cut.
- The brief's upper bound of 130% is not supported by any source found; the documented range is 108–125%. A 4K vertical source (2160×3840) allows up to 200% scale on a 1080×1920 timeline before upscaling, so resolution is not the limiting factor; framing is.
- No source gives a rate for shot-size changes. If every hidden jump cut alternates between two scales, shot size changes at the rate of surviving jump cuts.

### Gaps
- No evidence links pause length, or punch-in size, to retention or completion.
- Default thresholds for Descript, Premiere Pro's text-based editing, CapCut and AutoCut were not found.
- auto-editor's current default threshold is unresolved (4% in old releases, decibel syntax now).
- No source gave a number for how often shot size should change.

## 3. Pattern interrupts: how often the picture changes, B-roll, layouts, and how long the face can stay off screen

### Takeaway
"Change the picture every 3–5 seconds" is a practitioner heuristic whose quoted retention lifts come from blogs and one vendor's internal sample of about 200 videos; the best-documented B-roll figure is a single vendor test in which 35–50% B-roll coverage raised watch time 15–25% over talking-head-only, with diminishing returns beyond. I found no evidence on how long the speaker's face can stay off screen, only that showing a person in the first three seconds helps early retention.

### Cited Findings

#### Interval between picture changes
- **[Practitioner]** Every 3–5 seconds. — [OpusClip](https://www.opus.pro/blog/tiktok-length-format-retention-data); [Fluxnote](https://fluxnote.io/guides/how-to-add-b-roll-to-talking-head-video)
- **[Single claim]** B-roll every 5–8 seconds; beyond 10 seconds without a visual change viewers are at risk. — [Argil](https://argil.ai/blog/what-is-b-roll-the-complete-guide-for-video-creators-in-2026)
- **[Single claim: page not pinned]** A visual shift every 1.5–2.5 seconds. — one of [Skool post](https://www.skool.com/lmgs-ai-academy/the-pattern-interrupt-why-your-videos-get-swiped?p=1f56ca82), [Socialync](https://www.socialync.io/blog/retention-techniques-keep-viewers-watching-2026)
- **[Unsourced number: page not pinned]** Videos with a pattern interrupt every 4 seconds average 58% retention against 41% for static talking heads of the same length; no sample size or method. — one of [Socialync](https://www.socialync.io/blog/retention-techniques-keep-viewers-watching-2026), [dev.to, "Hook Engineering"](https://dev.to/bulkdl/hook-engineering-the-science-of-the-first-3-seconds-in-short-form-video-2026-2jp9)
- **[Single claim: vendor internal data, 200+ TikToks, page not pinned]** Interrupts every 3–5 seconds show 40–60% higher average retention. — one of [Edición Video Pro](https://edicionvideopro.com/?p=2185), [Socialync](https://www.socialync.io/blog/retention-techniques-keep-viewers-watching-2026)
- **[Unsourced number]** An agency cites "Facebook marketing data" that effective pattern interrupts in the first three seconds bring engagement up to three times higher; the underlying source could not be found. — probably [Inceptly newsletter](https://inceptly-agency.beehiiv.com/p/pattern-interrupts-simple-video-tricks-that-stop-the-scroll)
- **[Practitioner: page not pinned]** Fixed intervals habituate: "if you use zoom every exact 3 seconds, the brain detects the pattern and stops reacting"; vary the interval. — one of [CapCut, pattern interrupt technique](https://www.capcut.com/create/pattern-interrupt-technique-short-form-video), [Edición Video Pro](https://edicionvideopro.com/?p=2185)
- **[Unsourced number]** About 65% of viewers still watching at three seconds is quoted as the mark of an effective hook in several guides, none with a source. — for example [dev.to, "Hook Engineering"](https://dev.to/bulkdl/hook-engineering-the-science-of-the-first-3-seconds-in-short-form-video-2026-2jp9)

#### How much B-roll
- **[Single claim: vendor test, 17 November 2025; sample size, platform and significance not shown]** B-roll covering roughly 35–50% of a video lifted watch time by about 15–25% over talking-head-only versions. The author had predicted 8–15% and concludes that diminishing returns set in quickly. One version drew 23% and 31% more comments than the other two. OpusClip sells an AI B-roll tool. — [OpusClip, "Does B-Roll Increase Watch Time? A Controlled Test"](https://www.opus.pro/blog/does-b-roll-increase-watch-time)
- **[Practitioner: vendor]** B-roll should punctuate, not dominate: about three to six cutaways of 2–4 seconds in a 60-second clip. — [OpusClip, AI B-roll generator](https://www.opus.pro/blog/ai-b-roll-generator)
- **[Disagreement]** B-roll share of runtime: 30–60% for explainers — [Faceless](https://faceless.so/blog/retention-first-editing-boost-short-form-watch-time); 50–70% for short-form — [Fluxnote](https://fluxnote.io/guides/how-to-add-b-roll-to-talking-head-video)
- **[Practitioner]** Place cutaways on the specific phrases that benefit from illustration, then return to the face. — [OpusClip, talking-head guide](https://www.opus.pro/blog/talking-head-videos-guide)

#### The face
- **[Study: vendor analysis of Facebook Reels; sample and method not shown]** Featuring a person within the first three seconds improves short-term retention. — [Emplifi, Facebook Reels data](https://emplifi.io/resources/facebook-reels-data/)
- **[Single claim]** The gain is put at about 10% for 10-second retention on Reels. — [TechJuice](https://www.techjuice.pk/?p=226042)

#### Layouts and on-screen text
- **[Official: TikTok]** Music, dynamic transitions, movement, text overlays and emojis are named as attention triggers. — [TikTok Creative Codes](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-AU/creative-codes)
- **[Official: Instagram Help Center]** Instagram aims to show fewer Reels that are "mostly covered by text". — [Instagram Help Center](https://help.instagram.com/1525585517644948)
- **[Practitioner: vendor, 2026]** One of the named 2026 formats pairs a presenter with a visual and uses clean voiceover with minimal music. — [OpusClip, "11 Editing Aesthetics"](https://www.opus.pro/blog/editing-aesthetics-dominating-short-form-2026)
- **[Study: vendor, 13.5 million clips, January–March 2026]** Captions appear in 80.2% of analysed clips and 78.6% use animated captions, so moving text is the default constant layer. — one of [OpusClip, "The Anatomy of a Viral TikTok in 2026"](https://www.opus.pro/blog/anatomy-of-a-viral-tiktok-2026), [OpusClip, "50 Video Editing Tips"](https://www.opus.pro/blog/video-editing-tips)

### Inferences
- The B-roll figures do not agree. Three to six cutaways of 2–4 seconds is 6–24 seconds per minute, or 10–40% coverage; the vendor test favours 35–50%; other guides say 30–70%. The overlap is roughly 30–50%, which keeps the face on screen for at least half the runtime.
- With no evidence on face-off-screen limits, the only defensible number is the cutaway length the guides use: 2–4 seconds, then back to the face. Longer full-screen graphics are untested, not disproven; if used, the graphic itself should keep changing at the 2–5 second cadence.
- Instagram's "mostly covered by text" wording is the one official constraint on full-screen typography: text-only frames should stay brief on Reels.
- Vary intervals; a metronomic zoom every three seconds is called out as self-defeating.

### Gaps
- No evidence on how long the speaker's face can be off screen before retention suffers.
- No data on how often split-screen, picture-in-picture, screenshots or full-screen graphics appear in top videos, or how they perform against each other.
- No effect sizes for kinetic typography beyond caption prevalence.
- Searches refused when the budget ran out: Meta's Reels creative guidance (which I expected to cover human presence and audio) and the details of Emplifi's analysis.

## 5. Sound: loudness targets, music-bed level, sound effects, trending versus original audio, and music licensing for business accounts

### Takeaway
Only YouTube has a well-documented normalization reference (−14 LUFS, since 2019); Meta confirms that Reels and Stories are loudness-managed at playback but publishes no number, TikTok publishes nothing, and the common "−14 LUFS integrated, −1 dBTP" target is community consensus with dissenters at −10 to −12, at −16, and one claim that TikTok does not normalize at all. Music beds are advised anywhere from 6 to 30 dB under the voice (most often 15–24 dB), business accounts face real music limits on TikTok and Instagram, and trending-versus-original audio has no rigorous reach data.

### Cited Findings

#### Loudness targets and normalization
- **[Third-party audio engineering, 2019]** YouTube changed its loudness reference to −14 LUFS in 2019; "Stats for nerds" values could differ from the old reference by up to 3 dB; the author advises against mastering everything to −14. — [MeterPlugs](https://www.meterplugs.com/blog/2019/09/18/youtube-changes-loudness-reference-to-14-lufs.html)
- **[Third-party audio engineering]** YouTube's "Stats for nerds" panel shows how far a video sits from the reference; content louder than the reference is turned down. — [Production Advice](https://productionadvice.co.uk/stats-for-nerds/); [Frame.io workflow guide](https://workflow.frame.io/guide/loudness-for-youtube)
- **[Single claim]** The only source that explicitly applies −14 LUFS to Shorts is a DJ-focused blog, which also suggests a slight mid-high boost for phone speakers. No official YouTube page confirming the Shorts target was found. — [DJ Master blog](https://dj-master-web.vercel.app/en/blog/youtube-loudness-standard-en)
- **[Official: Meta engineering, April 2023]** Meta deployed the xHE-AAC codec on Reels and Stories. Its loudness management "solves for loudness inconsistency while meticulously preserving creator intent", evens out average loudness across sessions and manages dynamic range. The target is applied at playback according to context, not baked into the file. No LUFS figure is published. — [Engineering at Meta](https://engineering.fb.com/2023/04/11/video-engineering/high-quality-audio-xhe-aac-codec-meta/); coverage: [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/meta-updates-audio-encoding-for-reels-and-stories-to-provide-more-volume-co/647414)
- **[Practitioner: community consensus, no official spec]** Reels and TikTok: −14 LUFS integrated with a −1 dBTP ceiling. — [Mixing and Mastering AI](https://mixingandmastering.ai/blog/lufs-for-tiktok-and-reels); [dev.to](https://dev.to/arthurvalle1_a2586dd2b4bc/mastering-for-reels-tiktok-and-youtube-shorts-the-lufs-14-standard-2ck5)
- **[Practitioner: caveat, page not pinned]** TikTok's −14 LUFS is "approximate, with no official spec published". — probably [Vitelnk LUFS calculator](https://vitelnk.com/tools/calc/lufs-calculator)
- **[Disagreement: single claim]** Instagram and TikTok "prefer louder mixes", closer to −12 to −10 LUFS. — [OpusClip](https://www.opus.pro/blog/best-loudness-normalizers)
- **[Disagreement: single claim, page not pinned]** Social platforms normalize around −16 LUFS, though many creators push louder. — probably [this loudness reference page](https://d1lex0gkhzvix9.cloudfront.net/tiktok-instagram-reels-loudness)
- **[Disagreement: single claim, no measurement shown]** TikTok is "the exception: no in-feed normalization", so a hotter master plays louder. — [Songbrain](https://www.songbrain.ai/guides/lufs-for-spotify-and-tiktok)

#### Music bed under the voice
- **[Standard: W3C accessibility technique; weaker sourcing than the other bullets]** Technique G56 is about mixing audio so that non-speech sounds are at least 20 dB lower than the speech. The search returned this page but its summary did not quote it; the 20 dB figure is the technique's title as I know it, so confirm it on the page. — [W3C WCAG 2.2 Technique G56](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/general/G56.html)
- **[Practitioner: page not pinned]** Music roughly 18–24 dB quieter than the voice while someone is speaking; 22–30 dB below narration for tutorials. — probably [Overseer OS](https://www.overseeros.com/blog/background-music-volume-under-voiceover)
- **[Practitioner: page not pinned]** About 15 dB below the voice as a first guess, then adjust by ear phrase by phrase. — one of [Descript](https://www.descript.com/blog/article/how-to-set-the-perfect-audio-levels-for-video), [Soundstripe](https://soundstripe.com/blogs/how-to-perfect-your-audio-levels)
- **[Practitioner: vendor, page not pinned]** Auto-ducking starting point for creators: duck 6–12 dB, attack 30–80 ms, release 250–700 ms. — probably [Mubert auto-duck](https://mubert.com/tools/fuse/features/auto-duck)
- **[Practitioner: absolute levels]** Dialogue at −12 to −6 dBFS, music at −25 to −18 dBFS. — [Adorama](https://www.adorama.com/alc/mastering-audio-levels/)

#### Sound-on behaviour
- **[Official: TikTok, research mostly from 2021]** 88% of TikTok users say sound is vital to the experience; 73% would "stop and look" at a sound-on ad; sound-on delivery gives a 55% lift in communicating new information; 50% say music makes content more uplifting and entertaining. Several figures cite a 2021 Kantar study. — [TikTok Creative Codes](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-AU/creative-codes); [Creative Codes PDF, May 2023](https://www.tiktok.com/business/library/Creative_Codes_May_2023.pdf)
- **[Official: Instagram Help Center]** Instagram aims to show fewer Reels that are "muted". — [Instagram Help Center](https://help.instagram.com/1525585517644948)

#### Trending audio versus original audio
- **[Study: Fanpage Karma, 2023; page not pinned]** Reels using songs already used in more than 100,000 Reels got 17% more reach and 180% more interactions than Reels with less-used songs. This compares popular with less popular songs, not trending with original audio. — relayed by one of [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/trending-reels-on-instagram/), [SocialPilot](https://www.socialpilot.co/fr/blog/instagram-reels-trends)
- **[Single claim: vendor, no method]** Reels with trending audio can have 29% greater reach and 42% more engagement than content without audio. — [Adobe Express](https://adobe.com/express/learn/blog/trending-audio-social-media-content)
- **[Single claim: vendor]** In one sample, the 36.7% of Reels using trending audio averaged about 13 million views against about 9 million for the rest. — [Manychat](https://manychat.com/blog/?p=18241)
- **[Unsourced number]** Original audio is listed at −22% initial reach. — [Outfame](https://www.outfame.com/blog/trending-sounds-boost-engagement)
- **[Practitioner]** Neither is universally better for reach. — [Jeff Bullas forum](https://www.jeffbullas.com/thread/does-the-algorithm-prioritize-original-audio-over-trending-sounds-for-reels-disc/)

#### Music licensing for business accounts
- **[Official: TikTok]** Businesses cannot use the general music library for commercial use and are directed to the Commercial Music Library, described as pre-cleared and holding 1 million songs. — [TikTok Ads Help, About the Commercial Music Library](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article?aid=10005905)
- **[Disagreement on size]** A vendor guide cites "over 600,000" tracks from TikTok's business blog. — [Soundstripe](https://www.soundstripe.com/blogs/tiktok-music-licensing-rules)
- **[Practitioner: music vendors]** The restriction covers organic posts that promote a product, service, business or sponsor, including sponsored posts from personal accounts. Commercial Music Library tracks are cleared for TikTok only. — [Soundstripe](https://www.soundstripe.com/blogs/why-can-i-only-use-commercial-sounds-on-tiktok); [Thematic](https://hellothematic.com/music-rules-tiktok-instagram-youtube/)
- **[Practitioner: music vendor help page]** A business can post music it licenses elsewhere if the licence covers commercial use on TikTok, and must complete TikTok's Music Usage Confirmation at upload. — [Epidemic Sound help](https://help.epidemicsound.com/hc/en-us/articles/26251264491794-How-to-Post-Commercial-Content-on-TikTok)
- **[Official: Instagram Help Center]** "Certain business accounts and certain types of posts" do not have access to the licensed music library, so that music is not used commercially; such accounts may be able to use Meta's Sound Collection. — [Instagram Help Center](https://help.instagram.com/ipad-app/402084904469945)
- **[Practitioner: vendors]** Meta's Sound Collection holds more than 14,000 royalty-free songs and sounds, licensed for commercial or non-commercial use only in content on Meta's products. — [Soundstripe](https://www.soundstripe.com/blogs/instagram-music-library); [Music Business Worldwide](https://musicbusinessworldwide.com/meta-offers-royalty-free-music-library-to-instagram-reels-advertisers)
- **[Official: YouTube Help]** Using music from the Shorts audio library avoids copyright claims. The summary of the page did not say whether that covers brand or sponsored content. — [YouTube Help, Music eligibility for Shorts](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13486873?hl=en)
- **[Official: YouTube Help; direction unconfirmed]** The Creator Music eligibility page has a clause about "commercial brands" whose channels are dedicated to promoting their goods or services. The search summary read it as eligibility, but the page is titled "eligibility and restrictions" and a distributor's help page says videos paid for by a brand and primarily promoting it are excluded. — [YouTube Help, Creator Music eligibility and restrictions](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/11623091); [TuneCore](https://support.tunecore.com/hc/en-us/articles/8326210419860-YouTube-Creator-Music)

### Inferences
- −14 LUFS integrated with a −1 dBTP ceiling is the lowest-risk single master: it matches YouTube's reference, and Meta manages loudness at playback either way. The open risk is TikTok. If TikTok does not normalize, a −14 master plays quieter than neighbours mastered at −10 to −12; no source measured this.
- A music bed 18–24 dB under the voice satisfies the practitioner range and the W3C 20 dB accessibility rule at once. The "6–12 dB duck" figure describes how far to lower music from its own level, not the final gap, so it does not contradict the larger numbers.
- For a business publishing the same edit on all three platforms, only original audio or independently licensed music is portable. Each platform library is cleared for that platform alone.
- On trending audio, every number is weak and none isolates original voice audio. The defensible position for talking-head content is that the voice track is the audio, and a quiet licensed bed is optional.
- Instagram's "muted" wording means a silent export is a documented distribution risk, even for caption-led videos.

### Gaps
- Sound effects (whoosh, pop, riser): types, frequency and any effect on retention were not searched before the budget ran out. No findings.
- No official LUFS or true-peak number from Instagram or TikTok, and no independent playback measurement of either, was found.
- No Instagram statistic on sound-on viewing was searched.
- A TikTok support page (returned in Finnish) appeared to say commercial use of everything in "the library" is allowed. It probably refers to the Commercial Music Library, but this was not confirmed. — page: [TikTok Support](https://support.tiktok.com/fi/business-and-creator/creator-and-business-accounts/commercial-use-of-music-on-tiktok)
- Which Instagram business accounts lose the licensed library is not specified in the sources found.
- Unverified leads (background knowledge, not checked this session): YouTube is generally described as turning loud content down but not turning quiet content up; YouTube's Shorts audio library is, as I recall, offered for personal, non-commercial use unless the uploader holds its own licence.

## 6. Video length versus retention and completion, by platform (2025–2026 data)

### Takeaway
The 2026 datasets disagree on the best Reels length because they measure different things: Buffer (2.7 million Reels, each account against its own baseline) finds reach peaks at 90–179 seconds and collapses past 180 seconds, while Socialinsider (6 million brand Reels, pooled) puts the best views and engagement at 45–60 seconds. TikTok data runs longer than habit suggests (viral-tier median 41 seconds; the most views on videos over three minutes in Buffer's data), YouTube Shorts has the weakest evidence, and almost no source reports completion or retention by length.

### Cited Findings

#### Instagram Reels
- **[Study: Buffer, about 2.7 million Reels published through Buffer, 1 January–15 August 2026; published November 2026]** Reach against each account's own baseline: +7.4% for 90–179 seconds, +2.7% for 30–59 seconds, and −49.3% for 180 seconds or more. Accounts were included only with at least three Reels in one length range and presence in at least two ranges. Buffer does not say how many accounts or which countries. — [Buffer, "How Long Should Instagram Reels Be? We Analyzed 2.7 Million Reels"](https://buffer.com/library/instagram-reels-length)
- **[Study: Buffer, about 2.5 million Reels, same period]** Engagement flips: Reels of 180 seconds or more beat their account baseline by 2.65 percentage points, and Buffer names "over 3 minutes" the best length for engagement. — [Buffer](https://buffer.com/library/instagram-reels-length)
- **[Study: Socialinsider, 6 million Reels from brands, January–June 2026]** 45–60 seconds is the starting range with the highest engagement and views. Socialinsider calls these aggregate numbers and advises checking an account's own breakdown. — [Socialinsider](https://www.socialinsider.io/blog/instagram-reels-length/)
- **[Conflict on sample size]** Another Socialinsider post cites 9,771,822 Reels from brands with 1,000 to 1 million followers, and a secondary write-up describes about 140,000 Reels from business accounts. - **[Study: Fanpage Karma, 32,000 TikToks, date not shown]** About 72% of TikToks are shorter than 60 seconds. — [Fanpage Karma](https://www.fanpagekarma.com/insights/optimal-tiktok-video-length/)

A second Metricool article gives about 33,000 views and 42.6% more than Reels, so the exact gap is uncertain. — among [Metricool, 2026 short-video report](https://metricool.com/short-video-report-2026), [Metricool, state of short-form](https://metricool.com/the-state-of-short-form-social-media/), [Merca2.0 coverage](https://www.merca20.com/tiktok-is-the-best-platform-for-starting-from-scratch-small-accounts-get-nearly-2500-views-per-video/)
- **[Study: Metricool 2026; page not pinned]** Accounts up to 2,000 followers average about 1,290 views on Shorts and about 1,480 on Instagram. Accounts over 1 million followers average under 12,000 on Shorts and over 400,000 on Reels and TikTok. TikTok leads on average views for every size except the largest, where Instagram leads. — probably [Metricool, "Are YouTube Shorts Worth It in 2026?"](https://metricool.com/are-youtube-shorts-worth-it/)
- **[Study: vidIQ, July 2026, 448,413 channels]** A typical first Short reached 1,362 views after 30 days against 180 for a typical first long-form upload. — [vidIQ Research](https://vidiq.com/research/youtube-first-10-videos-study/)

### Inferences
- Reels, reach to new viewers: 30–60 seconds is the one band both 2026 studies favour (Buffer +2.7% at 30–59 seconds; Socialinsider best at 45–60 seconds). 90–179 seconds is Buffer's best band, but only for accounts whose content holds attention that long. Past 180 seconds reach roughly halves.
- Reels, engagement from existing followers: long videos (180 seconds or more) do best in Buffer's data, at the cost of reach. Reach and engagement are opposing goals at that length.
- TikTok: 30–45 seconds for discovery (viral-tier median 41 seconds, ad guidance 21–34 seconds); beyond 60 seconds when the goal is total views or watch time and the content can hold it.
- Shorts: under 30 seconds if the goal is retention percentage and replays; 30–60 seconds is the creators' other choice for subscriber growth. No performance dataset supports either.
- For one talking-head educational edit published to all three, 30–60 seconds is the overlap.
- The Buffer and Socialinsider results are not contradictory in method: one compares an account with itself, the other pools brands. A report should present both and say so.

### Gaps
- Completion rate and retention by length: no 2025–2026 dataset found. Every study above reports views, reach or engagement.
- Dash Social, Hootsuite and Wistia length data were planned but not searched before the budget ran out.
- No official TikTok statement on organic length was retrieved.
- Unverified leads (background knowledge, not checked this session): TikTok's Creator Rewards Program pays only on videos of one minute or longer; Instagram has said Reels longer than three minutes are not recommended to non-followers, which would explain Buffer's −49.3%.

## 7. Technical export specs recommended by each platform, and known quality or distribution penalties

### Takeaway
All three platforms accept H.264 MP4 at 1080×1920, but the published numbers differ: YouTube recommends 8 Mbps (24–30 fps) or 12 Mbps (48–60 fps) for 1080p, Instagram's API caps video at 25 Mbps and 1920 pixels wide with 23–60 fps, and TikTok accepts 23–60 fps and 360–4096 pixels per side with no bitrate recommendation for organic uploads. The documented penalties are Instagram's: fewer recommendations for Reels that are muted, blurry, low-resolution, watermarked, bordered or mostly covered by text; loss of recommendations for accounts that mostly repost; and lower-quality encodes for videos that stop getting views.

### Cited Findings

#### YouTube (applies to Shorts uploads)
- **[Official: YouTube Help; search metadata suggested the page may be several years old]** Recommended 1080p bitrates for standard dynamic range: 8 Mbps at 24, 25 or 30 fps; 12 Mbps at 48, 50 or 60 fps. For HDR: 10 and 15 Mbps. These are recommendations, not caps. — [YouTube Help, recommended upload encoding settings](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1722171?hl=en)
- **[Official: YouTube Help]** Codec H.264, High Profile, progressive scan, CABAC, variable bitrate, two consecutive B-frames, closed GOP of half the frame rate. Container MP4 with the moov atom at the front. Audio AAC-LC at 48 kHz, stereo. Upload at the frame rate recorded; deinterlace interlaced footage. — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1722171?hl=en)

#### Instagram Reels
- **[Official: Instagram Help Center; page last updated roughly early 2025]** Aspect ratio between 1.91:1 and 9:16; minimum 30 fps; minimum resolution 720 pixels. — [Instagram Help Center, Reel size and aspect ratios](https://help.instagram.com/1038071743007909)
- **[Official: Meta developer documentation; conflicts with the Help Center on frame rate]** For Reels published through the API: 23–60 fps, maximum width 1920 pixels, maximum video bitrate 25 Mbps, maximum duration 15 minutes, maximum file size 300 MB, 9:16 recommended to avoid cropping. — [Meta developer documentation, IG User Media](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/instagram-platform/instagram-graph-api/reference/ig-user/media)

#### TikTok
- **[Official: TikTok developer documentation]** MP4 recommended (WebM and MOV accepted); H.264 recommended (H.265, VP8 and VP9 supported); 23–60 fps; each dimension between 360 and 4096 pixels; maximum 4 GB; maximum 10 minutes through the upload API. — [TikTok Content Posting API, media transfer guide](https://developers.tiktok.com/docs/en/content-posting-api-media-transfer-guide?enter_method=left_navigation)
- **[Official: TikTok Creative Codes]** Frame at 9:16, use footage of 720p or higher, and leave room for the interface. — [TikTok Creative Codes](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-AU/creative-codes)
- **[Official: TikTok ad specs, not organic]** Reservation in-feed ads: 9:16 at 540×960 or larger, up to 500 MB, bitrate at least 2,500 kbps. Other ad products list minimums as low as 516 kbps. — [TikTok Ads Help](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/tiktok-reservation-in-feed-ads-reach-frequency?lang=en)
- **[Single claims: creators disagree]** One editor exports H.264 at 1080×1920, 25 fps, two-pass variable bitrate with a 20 Mbps target and 24 Mbps maximum. — [TikTok creator post](https://www.tiktok.com/@danielgrindrod/video/7278726009237638432?lang=en). Another says to upload 4K at a "50–100" bitrate and calls 1080p a misconception. — [TikTok creator post](https://www.tiktok.com/@itsn3t/video/7419017390039616774?lang=en). A third recommends 10–15 Mbps H.264 at 1080p, not 4K, claiming TikTok's player is optimised for 1080p. — page not pinned; probably [TikTok discover page](https://www.tiktok.com/discover/how-to-upload-4k-video-quality-tiktok)

#### Quality and distribution penalties
- **[Official: Instagram Help Center]** Instagram aims to show fewer Reels that are "muted, blurry, or low resolution", that "contain borders, logos, or watermarks", or that are "mostly covered by text". Reels from followed accounts are still shown in feed and on the profile and are not made less visible there. — [Instagram Help Center](https://help.instagram.com/1525585517644948)
- **[Official via press, February 2021]** Instagram announced that Reels carrying the TikTok logo would be de-emphasised in suggestions; a spokesperson said people have "a less satisfying experience" with content recycled from other apps or that is blurry and low resolution. — [Tubefilter, 10 February 2021](https://tubefilter.com/2021/02/10/instagram-reels-with-tiktok-watermark-less-discoverable/)
- **[Official: Instagram creators blog]** When Instagram detects identical content, it replaces reposts with the original in recommendations; accounts that repeatedly post unoriginal content can lose Reels recommendations. — [Instagram creators blog](https://creators.instagram.com/blog/rewarding-original-creators-on-instagram)
- **[Official via press, 30 April 2026]** The originality rules were extended to photos and carousels. Accounts that re-upload others' work are no longer eligible for recommendations; eligibility is assessed over 30 days and shown in Account Status. — [PetaPixel](https://petapixel.com/2026-04-30/new-instagram-policies-target-reposted-content/); [Tubefilter](https://tubefilter.com/2026/04/30/instagram-removes-algorithm-recommendations-repost-content-aggregator)
- **[Official via press: page not pinned, date not shown]** Instagram has tested alerts that tell creators when a Reel's reach may be limited because of a watermark. — one of the TechCrunch reports returned for the query: [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=2697938), [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=2551088)
- **[Official via press, October 2024]** Mosseri: a video not watched for a long time is moved to a lower-quality encode and re-rendered at higher quality if it is watched a lot again. The system works in aggregate on a sliding scale, and Instagram biases toward higher quality, which costs more processing and storage, for creators who drive more views. He called the difference small and said engagement depends far more on content than on quality. Slow connections also trigger lower-quality playback. — [TechCrunch, 27 October 2024](https://techcrunch.com/2024/10/27/instagram-is-lowering-video-quality-for-unpopular-videos); [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/social-media/mosseri-confirms-instagram-reduces-video-quality-for-posts-that-arent-raking-in-views-233033536.html)

### Inferences
- One export preset satisfies every published requirement:

| Setting | Value | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Frame size | 1080×1920 (9:16) | Within Instagram's 1920-pixel width cap and TikTok's range; above every stated minimum |
| Frame rate | 30 fps | Instagram's Help Center says 30 fps minimum; a 25 fps export meets the API range but not that wording |
| Codec and container | H.264 High Profile in MP4, moov atom at the front | Recommended by YouTube and TikTok; accepted by Instagram |
| Video bitrate | 10–20 Mbps, variable | Above YouTube's 8 Mbps recommendation, below Instagram's 25 Mbps cap |
| Audio | AAC-LC, 48 kHz, stereo | YouTube's stated recommendation |
| File size | Under 300 MB | Instagram's API limit is the tightest |

- Instagram keeps several encodes per video and serves lower-quality ones to videos that stop getting views, so on Instagram the delivered quality depends on views as well as on the upload. No source found documents a benefit from very high upload bitrates, and the creator claims that 4K uploads look better on TikTok are unverified and contradict each other.
- The penalties with official wording behind them are about recycled content and legibility, not bitrate: no third-party watermark, no borders, no silent export, no frame mostly covered by text, at least 720p.

### Gaps
- No official TikTok bitrate or resolution recommendation for organic uploads was found; the only TikTok bitrate figures are ad specifications and creators' posts.
- Colour space, HDR handling and 4K behaviour were not searched. Unverified lead (background knowledge, not checked this session): YouTube's encoding page recommends BT.709 for standard dynamic range.
- No source measured the bitrates the platforms deliver after re-encoding.
- Third-party watermark policies on YouTube Shorts and TikTok were not searched; only Instagram's policy was found.
- In-app "upload in HD" or "highest quality" settings on Instagram and TikTok were not searched.
- Unverified lead (background knowledge, not checked this session): Meta's API page also specifies AAC audio up to 48 kHz, closed GOP, 4:2:0 chroma and progressive scan.

## 8. Endings: loops, hard stops and end cards

### Takeaway
Evidence on endings is anecdotal: replays count as views on all three platforms (on Shorts since 31 March 2025), but YouTube's "engaged views", the metric tied to monetisation, did not change, and vidIQ cautions that loops do not reliably multiply watch time. The loop statistics in circulation are unsourced, and the only official guidance found is TikTok's hook, body, close structure ending in a strong call to action.

### Cited Findings
- **[Official via press, March 2025]** From 31 March 2025 a Shorts view counts each time a Short starts playing or replays, with no minimum watch time. The previous metric was renamed "engaged views" (visible in Advanced Mode), and YouTube Partner Program earnings and eligibility stay tied to engaged views. The change aligns Shorts with TikTok and Reels, which count starts and replays. — [TechCrunch, 26 March 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/26/youtube-is-changing-how-youtube-shorts-views-are-counted); [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-changes-shorts-view-counts-no-change-to-monetization/543005/)
- **[Practitioner: vidIQ]** Looping can add watch time, but it does not guarantee doubling it and does not create extra engaged views. — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/double-youtube-watch-time-looping-shorts)
- **[Official, secondhand; report not found]** YouTube's 2021 Shorts Report is said to recommend that the ending flow into the beginning. — relayed by [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/double-youtube-watch-time-looping-shorts)
- **[Practitioner: creator guide]** Short Shorts that end cleanly often get replayed, which can push retention above 100%; this is common under 30 seconds. — [Toptal](https://www.toptal.com/creator/post/youtube-shorts-length)
- **[Single claim: one creator's anecdote]** Trimming one second from the end of a Short raised retention from 83% to 88%. — [Toptal](https://www.toptal.com/creator/post/youtube-shorts-length)
- **[Single claim: one creator's anecdote]** A dead stop at the end hurt rewatches; adding a short callback line (a "button") raised them. — [The Canary](https://www.thecanary.co/?p=1749824)
- **[Unsourced numbers]** "84% greater algorithmic boost from rewatches" and "30% higher view counts from loops" appear on a vendor blog with no source. Do not use. — [Virvid](https://virvid.ai/blog/shorts-retention-blueprint-ai-data-signals)
- **[Official: TikTok Creative Codes]** "The best TikTok videos follow a reliable structure: hook, body, close"; the close should carry a strong call to action. — [TikTok Creative Codes](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-AU/creative-codes)
- **[Study: vendor, 13.5 million clips]** Branded intros appear in about 1% of analysed clips, which shows how rare bumper-style branding is at the start; no equivalent figure for end cards was found. — one of [OpusClip, "50 Video Editing Tips"](https://www.opus.pro/blog/video-editing-tips), [OpusClip, "The Anatomy of a Viral TikTok in 2026"](https://www.opus.pro/blog/anatomy-of-a-viral-tiktok-2026)

### Inferences
- Loops raise public view counts and retention percentage on every platform, but on YouTube they do not raise the engaged views that monetisation uses. A loop is a retention device, not a revenue one.
- The two anecdotes point the same way: cut the tail. Ending on the last useful word, or on a short line that hands back to the opening, is better supported than a pause, an outro or a logo hold, though only by single creators' reports.
- TikTok's official structure puts the call to action inside the close. That argues for a spoken or captioned call to action in the final beat, not for a separate static end card; no data compares the two.

### Gaps
- End cards: no data on static branded end cards against none, or on where a call to action performs best. Planned searches on TikTok and Meta end-card guidance were not run before the budget ran out.
- No loop or replay data specific to Instagram or TikTok was found.
- No controlled comparison of loop endings, hard stops and outros on any platform was found.
