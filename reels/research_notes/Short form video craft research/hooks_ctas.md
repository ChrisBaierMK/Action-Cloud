## 1. What the data says about the first 1–3 seconds, and how it relates to distribution

### Takeaway
No platform publishes a pass mark, but three numbers have a stated sample behind them: business Reels lose about 60–65% of initial views inside 3 seconds (Socialinsider, about 140K Reels, first half of 2026); Shorts with under 60% "viewed" rarely performed and the best sat at 70–90% (Paddy Galloway, 5,400 Shorts, 2023, self-described as preliminary); Meta sales ads average about 25% 3-second hook rate, with 30–40% called strong (Billo, 88,329 ads, first half of 2026; Motion). Officially, Instagram and TikTok say the first 3 to 6 seconds matter most, but no platform states a hold percentage that unlocks distribution. Every such threshold in circulation is lore.

### Cited Findings

**Instagram**
- **[Official, via TechCrunch, 2025-01-29]** Instagram began rolling out "View Rate" for Reels: the share of viewers (followers and non-followers) who keep watching past the 3-second mark. Instagram's stated reason: the first few seconds of a reel are the most important. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/01/29/instagram-gives-creators-more-insight-into-their-reels-performance)
- **[Official, via trade press, about August 2025]** Instagram added a per-reel Retention chart (share of views still watching at each moment) and "Skip Rate", which replaces View Rate. Skip rate is the share of views from people who skipped the reel during the first 3 seconds: views skipped in the first 3 seconds divided by initial views. Instagram's explanation as relayed: "a high Skip Rate indicates that people did not find the beginning of the reel engaging, whereas a low Skip Rate allows you to understand that your hook—or the first three seconds of the reel—resonated and kept people watching." — [Marketing4eCommerce](https://marketing4ecommerce.net/en/retention-and-skip-rate-reels/); [Metricool](https://metricool.com/instagram-reel-analytics/); [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-adds-retention-insights-reels/758464/)
- **[Official, 2025-12-03]** Meta's developer blog lists Reels Skip Rate among new Instagram API Insights metrics, so third-party tools can now report it. — [Meta for Developers](https://developers.facebook.com/blog/post/2025/12/03/instragram-api-updates/)
- **[Large sample, vendor, secondary]** Socialinsider analysed about 140K business Reels (January–June 2026). Skip rate by account size: 1–5K followers 65.50%; 5–10K 65.30%; 10–50K 63.40%; 50–100K 62.60%; 100K–1M 60.50%. Larger accounts skip less. Write-ups of the same study give slightly different figures, and I could not check how the study defines the metric. — [Socialinsider](https://www.socialinsider.io/blog/instagram-reels-statistics/); [Influencer Marketing Hub](https://influencermarketinghub.com/instagram-reels-stats/)
- **[Large sample, vendor, secondary]** Same study: accounts with 1–5K followers averaged a 9.78% reel reach rate against 5.00% for 100K–1M accounts. — [Influencer Marketing Hub](https://influencermarketinghub.com/instagram-reels-stats/)
- **[Practitioner]** Meta publishes no skip-rate benchmark. Guides say the only valid comparison is your own back catalogue, and "good" means falling over time on your account. — [Lilach Bullock](https://www.lilachbullock.com/what-is-a-good-skip-rate-on-instagram/); [Adaptly](https://adaptlypost.com/blog/reels-skip-rate)
- **[Official statement, secondary, January 2025]** Adam Mosseri: "The top three signals that matter most for ranking are watch time, likes and sends." He advised watching average watch time, likes per reach and sends per reach. Skip rate and view rate were not named as ranking signals in anything I found; they are presented as diagnostics of the hook. — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-shares-algorithm-insights-2025/738034/)

**YouTube Shorts**
- **[Official]** YouTube Help defines "Viewed vs. swiped away" as the percentage of times viewers viewed your Shorts versus swiped away. — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12220281)
- **[Official by absence, secondary]** YouTube publishes no benchmark or cutoff for this metric or for Shorts retention. Any table of targets is creator-reported. — [Creator Essentials](https://www.creatoressentials.com/glossary/viewed-vs-swiped-away/); [1of10](https://1of10.com/blog/youtube-shorts-analytics/)
- **[Practitioner]** Creator-reported bands: 70–79% viewed is the middle range and 80% or more is strong (1of10); aim for at least 75–80% (Subscribr); at minimum, Viewed should exceed Swiped Away, and you should set your own channel baseline (Social Media Examiner). — [1of10](https://1of10.com/blog/youtube-shorts-analytics/); [Subscribr](https://subscribr.ai/p/youtube-shorts-analytics-metrics-growth); [Social Media Examiner](https://www.socialmediaexaminer.com/how-to-analyze-your-youtube-shorts/)
- **[Large sample, practitioner-run, dated: April 2023]** Paddy Galloway and analyst Chris Gileta studied 3.3 billion views across 33 channels and 5,400 Shorts. "Shorts with under 60% VVSA rarely performed well (red). The best performing shorts were typically between 70 and 90…" He called these "initial results (subject to more testing)". The takeaway reported from the thread: make the first second punchy. — [Galloway on X](https://x.com/PaddyG96/status/1646898368495382528); [thread start](https://x.com/PaddyG96/status/1646898356419981315); [Digital Information World](https://www.digitalinformationworld.com/2023/04/paddy-galloway-decoded-youtube-shorts.html)
- **[Official, via TechCrunch, 2025-03-26]** From March 31, 2025 a Shorts view counts when a Short starts to play or replays, with no minimum watch time. The old metric lives on as "engaged views". YouTube says Partner Program earnings and eligibility still use engaged views. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/26/youtube-is-changing-how-youtube-shorts-views-are-counted); [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtubecreatorstudio/answer/2991785?hl=en) (probable help page for the earnings statement; search did not confirm which)
- **[Official, single search hit, not corroborated]** A YouTube blog post dated 2026-08-19 says that from 2026-08-24 views across all of YouTube are counted from the first frame, and the previous definition is now called an "engaged view". Search found no second source. — [YouTube Blog](https://blog.youtube/inside-youtube/engaged-views-youtube-explained/)
- **[Official, via TechCrunch, dated: August 2023]** Todd Sherman, then Shorts product lead, said the Shorts algorithm differs from long-form, "where people are tapping on videos to watch": in the Shorts feed the viewer swipes instead of choosing. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/08/25/youtube-demystifies-the-shorts-algorithm-views-and-answers-other-creator-questions/)
- **[Official, secondary, 2025]** Todd Beaupré (YouTube recommendations) points to viewer satisfaction, measured through direct surveys and other feedback signals, as what the system optimises. — [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/how-youtubes-recommendation-system-works-in-2025/538379/)

**TikTok**
- **[Official, advertising guidance]** "Introduce your content proposition in the first 3 seconds for better recall and awareness." "Prioritize your hook in the first 6 seconds to boost engagement and increase watch time. You can do so by creating suspense, surprise, or other emotions." — [TikTok Business Help Center](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/creative-best-practices?lang=en)
- **[Official, advertising guidance]** "The Hook: The first 3-6 seconds of your video are crucial. Captivate your potential customers with a powerful, attention-grabbing hook." — [TikTok SMB Creative Playbook](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/library/SMB_Creative_Playbook.pdf)
- **[Official, platform's internal research, dated: May 2023]** 90% of ad recall impact and 80% of awareness impact are captured within the first 6 seconds. Sample not stated in what search returned. — [TikTok Creative Codes](https://www.tiktok.com/business/library/Creative_Codes_May_2023.pdf)
- **[Large sample, vendor with platform, ads, 2023 data]** VidMob analysed 1,678 TikTok ads and 7.3 billion impressions (January–October 2023), looking at the first six seconds. Its recommended hook strategies were associated with 2x engagement and 43% higher purchase intent. Ads showing both a person and a product in the first six seconds had a 5% lift in 6-second views against average. — [Adweek wire](https://www.adweek.com/adweek-wire/report-reveals-key-visual-and-creative-ad-strategies-for-hooking-tiktok-audiences/); [VidMob report PDF](https://www.vidmob.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/Vidmob-x-Tik-Tok-Hook-Analysis-.pdf)
- **[Official, transparency page]** TikTok's system produces a prediction score for each video estimating how likely you are to interact with it; user interactions are the main input. No hold-rate figure is published. — [TikTok transparency](https://www.tiktok.com/transparency/recommendation-system/?lang=hi); [TikTok Newsroom](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/en-us/how-tiktok-recommends-videos-for-you/)

**Paid social "hook rate" (3-second plays divided by impressions)**
- **[Practitioner]** Hook rate is a custom calculation, not a metric Meta reports. Motion standardises it as 3-second views divided by impressions. Some tools divide ThruPlays by impressions instead, which gives a much smaller number. TikTok uses a 2-second view, so TikTok and Meta hook rates are not comparable. — [Motion help](https://help.motionapp.com/en/articles/8991407-motion-metrics-for-meta-and-tiktok); [Zeely](https://zeely.ai/blog/ways-to-boost-your-meta-ads-hook-rate/); [Billo](https://billo.app/blog/hook-rate-to-hold-rate) (these points came from several vendor explainers; search did not map each point to one page)
- **[Large sample, vendor, first half of 2026]** Billo: 88,329 sales-objective Meta video ads averaged a 25.44% hook rate, up from 24.42% in the second half of 2025. No industry averaged above 29%. Range: 21.32% (pet supplies) to 28.79% (toys and games); apparel 22.94%; health and beauty 28.11%. Cold and warm traffic are mixed and there is no placement split. — [Billo data page](https://billo.app/creative-insider/data-insights/hook-rate-benchmarks/); [Billo blog](https://billo.app/blog/hook-rate-to-hold-rate); [Top Growth Marketing](https://topgrowthmarketing.com/tools/hook-rate-calculator/)
- **[Practitioner, vendor rule of thumb]** Motion: strong Meta creative typically hits a 30–40% hook rate, a 25% or higher hold rate, and 1.5% or higher click-through rate for e-commerce. — [Motion](https://motionapp.com/blog/key-creative-performance-metrics)
- **[Single claims, vendors, they disagree]** Healthy cold-audience Meta hook rate 20–35%, above 40% elite (Imagine.art); 15–35% (Kickbite). Billo suggests 30% as the bar on Meta and reads a high hook rate with under 25% hold as a pacing problem. — [Imagine.art](https://www.imagine.art/blogs/what-is-hook-rate-improve-ad-creative); [Kickbite](https://www.kickbite.io/en/blog/hook-rate); [Billo](https://billo.app/blog/hook-rate-to-hold-rate)

### Inferences
- Working benchmark table, built from the findings above. It is a synthesis, not a published standard.

| Metric | Typical | Good | Great | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Instagram skip rate (share of views lost in 3 seconds) | 60–65% for business accounts | Below your own median; under about 60% | Unknown: no top-decile data found | Socialinsider, about 140K Reels |
| YouTube Shorts "viewed" share | Under 60% rarely performs | 70–79% | 80% or more; best performers 70–90% | Galloway 2023 (5,400 Shorts); creator-reported tables |
| Meta ad hook rate (3-second plays ÷ impressions) | About 25% | 30% or more | 40% or more | Billo (88,329 ads); Motion; vendor convention |
| Hold rate after the hook (ads) | Not found | 25% or more | Not found | Motion rule of thumb |

- A 60–65% skip rate implies that only about 35–40% of a typical business reel's initial views survive 3 seconds, if skip rate and the old view rate are complements. That is my arithmetic, not a published figure.
- These metrics cannot be compared across platforms. Instagram divides by initial views, YouTube by times shown in the feed, Meta ads by impressions, and TikTok ads use a 2-second view.
- Because public Shorts views now count any play start (March 2025), view counts say nothing about the hook. Use engaged views and viewed vs swiped away instead.
- On the link to distribution: Instagram and TikTok describe ranking in terms of watch time and interactions, not a named 3-second gate. The only public quantified link between an opening-seconds metric and performance is Galloway's 2023 correlation for Shorts.

- No platform states a hold percentage that triggers wider distribution. I found no official number for any of the three.
- No large-sample TikTok organic benchmark for 2-second or 3-second hold. TikTok's own figures are for ads.
- No public second-by-second drop-off curves from a large sample. Instagram's retention chart is per reel and visible only to the owner.
- Planned searches that did not run: Metricool's 2026 short-form study; Buffer's 2026 engagement study; the Bytecap "YouTube Shorts retention benchmarks" research page (seen by title only at https://www.bytecap.io/research/youtube-shorts-retention-benchmarks).

- Unverified leads from my background knowledge (check before use): Facebook for Business reported in 2016 that 65% of people who watch the first 3 seconds of a video watch at least 10 seconds and 45% watch 30 seconds (Facebook feed video, not Reels). Instagram's "Trial reels" (December 2024) show a reel to non-followers first, which is a native way to test hook variants.

## 2. Hook taxonomies and formulas, and which have large-sample support

### Takeaway
Large-sample support exists mainly for three opening types, all from one vendor's correlational data (OpusClip, January–March 2026): show the product or outcome first (TikTok), lead with expertise or credibility (YouTube), and direct address, intrigue or question (Instagram). For paid ads, Motion's 578,750-creative study favours newness, offer, price and urgency openers, with curiosity close behind. Contrarian, list, negative-warning and story-in-the-middle hooks rest on practitioner frameworks only in what I could retrieve. I could not collect sourced word-for-word examples from named videos: the pages that hold them were blocked.

### Cited Findings

**Large-sample analyses**
- **[Large sample, vendor, January–March 2026]** OpusClip's research pages draw on 13.5M+ clips processed by its engine, with hook-type rankings from subsets: 34,635 clips (TikTok), 8,254 clips (YouTube), 952,227 clips (Instagram). The pages do not define "viral" beyond view counts, and the findings are correlational. — [OpusClip TikTok hooks](https://www.opus.pro/research/best-video-hooks-tiktok); [OpusClip YouTube hooks](https://www.opus.pro/research/best-video-hooks-youtube); [OpusClip Instagram hooks](https://www.opus.pro/research/best-video-hooks-instagram)
- **[Large sample, vendor]** TikTok: "Project or Product Showcase" hooks (called "Product/Outcome Showcase" elsewhere on the site) have the highest average views, 6,037 per clip, about 2x the lowest-ranked hook type. — [OpusClip TikTok hooks](https://www.opus.pro/research/best-video-hooks-tiktok); [OpusClip blog, April 2026](https://www.opus.pro/blog/anatomy-of-a-viral-tiktok-2026)
- **[Large sample, vendor]** TikTok, lower-ranked types: Problem/Solution Setup hooks average 2,034 views and Shock/Surprise hooks 1,973. — [OpusClip problem/solution](https://www.opus.pro/research/how-to-use-problem-solution-setup-hooks); [OpusClip shock/surprise](https://www.opus.pro/research/how-to-use-shock-surprise-hooks)
- **[Large sample, vendor, platform unclear]** "Absurd Statement" hooks average 4,452 views. The figure comes from a repurposing guide and I could not tell which platform it covers. — [OpusClip repurposing guide](https://www.opus.pro/research/repurpose-tiktok-for-youtube)
- **[Large sample, vendor]** YouTube: the top hook type is expertise or credibility, at 6,706 average views. — [OpusClip YouTube hooks](https://www.opus.pro/research/best-video-hooks-youtube)
- **[Large sample, vendor]** Instagram: Direct Address / Intrigue / Question hooks drive the most engagement. — [OpusClip Instagram](https://www.opus.pro/research/how-to-go-viral-instagram); [OpusClip Instagram hooks](https://www.opus.pro/research/best-video-hooks-instagram)
- **[Large sample, vendor, paid Meta ads, September 2025–January 2026]** Motion's Creative Benchmarks 2026: 578,750 creatives, 6,015 advertiser accounts, about $1.29 billion in Meta spend. It ranks 25+ hook and headline tactics (only those used by 50 or more accounts) by "hit rate" (share of creatives that become winners) and spend use ratio. Newness, Sale Announcement, Price Anchor and Urgency lead on hit rate. Motion warns the window was dominated by promotional cycles, so a non-promotional period would likely compress the advantage. Findings are associations, not causal claims, and do not measure return on ad spend. — [Motion: top hook tactics](https://motionapp.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/top-hook-tactics); [Motion report PDF](https://content.motionapp.com/hubfs/Benchmarks-Report-2026.pdf)
- **[Secondary readings of the Motion report, they conflict]** Heista: hit rates run roughly 6% to 11% across 19 hook types, and Curiosity sits in the high tier with a high spend use ratio in every vertical. Vistalis: the report gives 9.29% for offer-only hooks and 8.74% for confessions, and publishes no hook-rate tiers. About 5% of all creatives become winners (3.8% at the smallest spend tier, 8.2% at the largest). — [Heista](https://www.heista.co/intelligence/creative-benchmarks-2026/hooks-that-convert); [Vistalis](https://www.vistalis.net/guides/hook-rate); [AdLiftr](https://adliftr.com/blog/ad-creative-testing-statistics-2026)
- **[Official, advertising guidance]** TikTok names suspense, surprise or other emotions as ways to build the hook in the first 6 seconds. — [TikTok Business Help Center](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/creative-best-practices?lang=en)

**Practitioner taxonomies (advice, not measured)**
- **[Practitioner, secondary]** Kallaway's three-step spoken formula: context lean-in, scroll-stop interjection, contrarian snapback. A related summary describes his contrast model: state the viewer's current belief (A), then set a contrarian alternative (B) against it. — [SozAI summary](https://sozai.app/transcript/psychology-killer-hooks/) (the formula is in one of three SozAI summaries of his videos; search did not say which); [Wayfind Substack](https://wayfindknow.substack.com/p/irresistible-hook)

The post does not credit a source for the six names. — [Timo Mason Substack](https://timomason.substack.com/p/how-to-create-scroll-stopping-hooks)
- **[Practitioner]** Verbal call-out taxonomy: labels that name the audience, yes-questions, if-then conditionals, and "ridiculous results" claims. — [Margin Notes](https://margin-notes-600.pages.dev/frameworks/verbal-callout-taxonomy)
- **[Practitioner, secondary]** A guide to Alex Hormozi's style recommends "How I" over "How to" phrasing, tied to a storytelling approach. — [OpusClip blog](https://opus.pro/blog/create-videos-in-alex-hormozi-style-the-ultimate-guide) (probable page; search described it only as "a video guide")
- **[Practitioner, secondary]** Jenny Hoyos wants a hook under three seconds, visually clear, understandable on mute, and one that foreshadows the payoff so viewers stay to the end. — [vidIQ interview summary](https://rosetta.to/u/vidiq/how-jenny-hoyos-actually-makes-viral-shorts)
- **[Single claim, anecdote]** A Telegram channel of hook anecdotes carries a post titled "I flipped the hook from 'how to' to 'stop doing'". Search also relays, from the same set of anecdotal posts, one account's rewatch rate rising from 4% to 21% after a hook change; it did not say which post. No method given. — [Telegram post](https://t.me/s/HookConfessions/11)

- OpusClip's averages are a few thousand views per clip, so the sample is ordinary users' clips cut from long videos, not top creators. It shows what works for typical accounts, which suits small businesses, but it should not be described as an analysis of viral hits.
- Motion's ranking measures which ads won budget during a sale-heavy season. It is not evidence about organic hooks, and offer-led openers are mostly irrelevant to service businesses without a price promotion.
- The one pattern that shows up in every dataset: start with the thing itself (outcome, product, proof, credential) and skip the set-up. Problem-first openings rank low in the TikTok data.
- Illustrative wording, written by me to match the pattern descriptions above. These are not quotations from measured videos and must not be presented as such.
  - Result first: "This kitchen took four days and came in 1,200 under the quote. Here's how." (finished kitchen in the first frame)
  - Yes-question: "Is your website quietly losing you bookings?"
  - Label call-out: "Physios with a waiting list: this is for you."
  - If-then call-out: "If your reels die at 200 views, check your first frame."
  - Curiosity gap: "One line on your invoice decides whether you get paid on time."
  - Contrarian: "Posting every day is why your account isn't growing."
  - List promise: "Three things I check before I quote any roof repair."
  - Negative or warning: "Stop sealing your deck until you've done this."
  - Story from the middle: "So the client calls at 6 a.m. and says the whole floor is under water."
  - "How I": "How I cut our no-shows in half with one text message."

## 3. The three layers of a hook: spoken line, first frame and motion, on-screen headline

### Takeaway
Practitioner agreement (Kallaway, Hoyos) is that the picture, the spoken line and the on-screen text must point at one subject and plant one question, land inside about 3 seconds, and work on mute. Platform ad data supports getting text on screen early (TikTok: text in the first 7 seconds, +43% conversion) and vendor data shows captions in about 80% of high-performing clips. Word and second counts for each layer are convention; I found almost no measurement of them.

### Cited Findings
- **[Practitioner, secondary]** Kallaway: three elements must agree: what appears on screen, what the creator says, and the text laid over the picture. Rule as summarised: "single subject, single question". If the three point to different ideas, viewers freeze or miss the content. — [Recapio summary](https://recapio.com/digest/how-to-create-a-killer-hook-impossible-to-skip-by-kallaway); [SozAI transcript summary](https://sozai.app/transcript/create-killer-hook-impossible-skip/)
- **[Practitioner, secondary]** Kallaway's working order: choose the question, sketch the visual, write the spoken line, run a one-subject check, then add text that matches the spoken line. — [Recapio summary](https://recapio.com/digest/how-to-create-a-killer-hook-impossible-to-skip-by-kallaway)
- **[Practitioner, conflicting, September 2026]** Another guide argues the text and the spoken line should complement each other instead of repeating. — [Jupitrr](https://jupitrr.com/how-to/write-hooks-for-talking-head-videos)
- **[Practitioner, secondary]** Hoyos: hook under three seconds, visually clear, understandable on mute. — [vidIQ interview summary](https://rosetta.to/u/vidiq/how-jenny-hoyos-actually-makes-viral-shorts)
- **[Official, advertising guidance]** TikTok: state the proposition in the first 3 seconds; build the hook in the first 6. — [TikTok Business Help Center](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/creative-best-practices?lang=en)
- **[Official, platform research, dated: December 2021]** TikTok: videos with on-screen text in the first 7 seconds saw a 43% lift in conversion against videos that showed text later. — [TikTok for Business blog](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-US/blog/creative-that-drives-conversions)
- **[Unverified, flagged]** Search attributes to TikTok's guidance a rate of "5-10 words per second" for on-screen text. That is faster than speech and I could not see the page, so treat the unit as suspect until checked. — [TikTok Business Help Center](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/creative-best-practices?lang=en)
- **[Large sample, vendor, timing claims vary by page]** OpusClip gives the deciding window as the first 1–2 seconds for Reels, the first 2 seconds for Shorts, and the first 3 seconds for TikTok (a product or outcome showcase). — [OpusClip Reels](https://www.opus.pro/research/how-to-go-viral-instagram-reels); [OpusClip Shorts](https://www.opus.pro/research/how-to-go-viral-youtube-shorts); [OpusClip TikTok](https://www.opus.pro/research/how-to-go-viral-tiktok)
- **[Large sample, vendor]** In the 13.5M-clip sample, 80.2% of viral clips use captions and 78.6% use caption animation; the Instagram page gives captions in 80% of viral clips. — [OpusClip viral video guide](https://www.opus.pro/research/how-to-make-viral-video) (probable page for the two exact figures; search did not confirm which); [OpusClip Instagram](https://www.opus.pro/research/how-to-go-viral-instagram)
- **[Large sample, vendor with platform, ads]** A person and a product together in the first six seconds: +5% 6-second views against average (1,678 TikTok ads, 2023). — [VidMob report PDF](https://www.vidmob.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/Vidmob-x-Tik-Tok-Hook-Analysis-.pdf)
- **[Vendor with platform, ads, dated: 2020]** Audio with 4 or more words per second showed a 19% lift in conversion rate against talk tracks at 2–3 words per second. On-screen talent showing 4 or more emotions had a 3.3x conversion rate against 3 or fewer. — [TikTok for Business blog on VidMob](https://www.tiktok.com/business/es/blog/vidmobs-data-driven-insights-to-supercharge-ad-creative-on-tiktok)
- **[Large sample, vendor, dated: 2021, not TikTok]** Across 1.1 million video assets on Instagram, Facebook, Snapchat and YouTube, a model's eye gaze in the first 2–3 seconds measurably changed performance, but the direction depended on context. — [GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2021/12/14/2352024/0/en/VidMob-Research-Reveals-That-Creative-Choices-Like-A-Model-s-Eye-Gaze-Have-Measurable-Impact-on-Ad-Performance.html)
- **[Secondary, Facebook only]** 74% of Facebook videos are watched without sound. — [Sprout Social](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/social-media-video-statistics/)
- **[Lore]** "68% of TikTok views and 54% of Reels views begin with sound off or very low": cited to an unnamed 2025 study. — [dev.to post](https://dev.to/bulkdl/hook-engineering-the-science-of-the-first-3-seconds-in-short-form-video-2026-2jp9)

### Inferences
- Length of each layer, derived by arithmetic from the cited windows, not from a study:
  - Spoken line: it has to finish inside about 3 seconds. At a brisk 3–4 words per second that is roughly 7 to 12 words. The 2020 VidMob finding favours the faster end.
  - On-screen headline: it has to be read in the first 1–2 seconds while the voice is starting, so about 3 to 7 words. It should be on screen from the first frame, well inside TikTok's 7-second finding.
  - First frame: it should already show the subject (outcome, product, face plus product), because the measured winners open on the thing itself and Galloway's advice is to make the first second punchy.
- How the layers should agree: one subject and one open question across all three. Whether the text repeats the spoken line word for word (Kallaway) or adds to it (Jupitrr) is unresolved; both camps agree the two must not introduce different ideas.
- Mistakes implied by these sources: three layers about three different things; a hook that needs sound to make sense; text that arrives after the viewer has decided; opening on set-up, greeting or logo instead of the outcome; a headline too long to read in the time it is shown.

### Gaps
- No measurement of first-frame motion (camera move, cut rate, movement in frame) was found. VidMob says it can compare two against three scene changes in the first three seconds but publishes no figure.
- No official Instagram or TikTok figure for sound-off viewing was found.
- Safe-zone and text-size specifications were not researched; another researcher may cover platform specifications.
- Kallaway's exact wording comes from third-party summaries of his videos, not his own text.
- Planned search that did not run: Meta's official Reels creative best practices and their statistics.

## 4. Holding attention after the hook: re-hooks, open loops, payoff placement, story structures and timings

### Takeaway
The measured part is length and hold: a 2023 Shorts study and 2026 vendor data both point to videos of roughly 30–60 seconds that keep people to the end, with the best Shorts holding past 50 seconds on average. The craft advice (open loops, "but" and "so" turns, payoff in the final second, pattern interrupts every few seconds) is practitioner consensus without published measurement, and "re-hook" has no agreed definition or timing.

### Cited Findings
- **[Large sample, practitioner-run, dated: April 2023]** Galloway: "Shorts where the AVD was higher than 50 seconds averaged 4.1 million views. Just making a long short isn't the goal, making a short that holds people for as long as possible is." Most Shorts in the study ran 20–40 seconds. The algorithm appeared to favour longer Shorts (40 seconds or more) that hold viewers, with outliers at every length. — [Galloway on X](https://x.com/PaddyG96/status/1646898363466481664); [length distribution](https://x.com/PaddyG96/status/1646898359808974851); [Digital Information World](https://www.digitalinformationworld.com/2023/04/paddy-galloway-decoded-youtube-shorts.html)
- **[Large sample, vendor, April 2026]** OpusClip: the viral-tier median length on TikTok is 41 seconds, 18% shorter than the overall median. — [OpusClip blog](https://www.opus.pro/blog/anatomy-of-a-viral-tiktok-2026)
- **[Vendor studies, secondary, 2026]** Write-ups of 2026 Reels studies, Socialinsider's among them: Reels of 30–60 seconds had the highest reach rate (5.60%); another write-up says 45–60 seconds had the highest engagement rate (0.35%). Search did not confirm that both figures come from the same study. — [Socialinsider](https://www.socialinsider.io/blog/instagram-reels-statistics/); [Influencer Marketing Hub](https://influencermarketinghub.com/instagram-reels-stats/)
- **[Official, secondary, dated: June 2024]** Instagram said at the time that Reels longer than 90 seconds can hurt reach. — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-clarifies-advice-single-word-ctas-longer-reels/718151/)
- **[Official]** Instagram's Retention chart shows where viewers leave within a reel, which is the tool for placing the next turn. — [Marketing4eCommerce](https://marketing4ecommerce.net/en/retention-and-skip-rate-reels/)
- **[Practitioner, secondary]** Hoyos: writes the hook and the last line first, then fills the middle; Shorts of about 34 seconds; the payoff sits in the last second; uses "but" and "so" to keep conflict and resolution moving; reportedly aims for 90% retention or higher. — [Leaderonomics](https://www.leaderonomics.com/videos/box-of-chocolates/how-to-make-great-youtube-shorts-lessons-from-jenny-hoyos); [vidIQ interview summary](https://rosetta.to/u/vidiq/how-jenny-hoyos-actually-makes-viral-shorts)
- **[Practitioner, conflicting]** In a podcast transcript Hoyos is quoted as saying retention "doesn't matter as much as people think it does". — [Creator Science podcast](https://podcast.creatorscience.com/jenny-hoyos/)
- **[Practitioner]** Open loops: a loop is an unanswered question planted in the viewer's mind; viewers leave when they feel it is resolved, so open a new one each time you close one. — [Adam Connell](https://adamconnell.me/improve-video-retention-social-media/); [Postiz](https://postiz.com/blog/why-your-reels-arent-going-viral-hook-framework) (search did not say which of the surfaced practitioner pages this wording is from)
- **[Single claim, template]** A timed opening template: 0–2 seconds pattern interrupt, 2–5 promise or benefit, 5–10 proof or preview. — [Skill plugin reference](https://skillselion.com/skills/josiahsiegel/claude-plugin-marketplace/viral-video-hooks-retention)
- **[Practitioner, vendor]** Diagnosis rule for ads: a high hook rate with under 25% hold is a pacing problem, not a hook problem. — [Billo](https://billo.app/blog/hook-rate-to-hold-rate)
- **[Lore]** "Videos with a pattern interrupt every 4 seconds held 58% on average against 41% for static talking heads (2026 analysis)". No method or sample given. — [AutomateEd](https://automateed.com/pattern-interrupt-ideas-for-short-form-content) (probable source; search did not confirm the page)

### Inferences
- A structure consistent with the cited sources, for a 30–45 second video. The timings are my synthesis.
  - 0–3 seconds: hook in all three layers; foreshadow the payoff.
  - 3–10 seconds: the stake or proof that makes the payoff worth waiting for.
  - Middle: steps or story beats joined by "but" and "so", each closing one question and opening the next.
  - Last 1–3 seconds: the payoff, then stop. If there is a call to action, it rides on the payoff or follows it immediately.
- "Re-hook" appears to be practitioner shorthand for opening a new loop when the previous one closes. I found no source that defines it or measures a best interval, so any "every N seconds" rule should be labelled as lore.
- Replays now count as Shorts views (March 2025) and Instagram ranks on watch time, so an ending that loops into the opening is rewarded on both. This is an inference from the counting rules, not a measured effect.
- Length advice has moved towards 30–60 seconds, but the evidence says hold matters more than length: Galloway's own caveat is that a long Short is not the goal.

### Gaps
- No large-sample measurement of payoff placement, loop frequency or pattern-interrupt cadence was found.
- No typical-timing data for story structures in top videos beyond Hoyos's 34 seconds and the length medians above.
- TikTok completion-rate and average-watch-time benchmarks were not found.
- The Hoyos material is all second-hand summaries; dates of the interviews were not captured.
- Unverified leads from my background knowledge: YouTube raised the Shorts limit to 3 minutes in October 2024 and Instagram extended Reels to 3 minutes in January 2025; if confirmed, the June 2024 "over 90 seconds hurts reach" guidance is out of date. The "but / so" device is usually credited to the South Park writers' "but / therefore" rule.

## 5. Calls to action: types, placement, wording, and conversion or engagement data

### Takeaway
The only hard call-to-action data is from TikTok's own ad research: a clear text call to action lifted conversion (figures from +44% to +152% across studies from 2020 to 2023), and showing it early beat showing it late. For organic video, one small study (540 Reels) found that calls to action were associated with worse performance overall, with comment prompts the exception. Comment-a-keyword-for-a-message has vendor-reported conversion of 12–20% but no independent benchmark.

### Cited Findings

**Measured effects (mostly paid, mostly dated)**
- **[Official, platform research, dated: December 2021]** TikTok: calls to action in text form showed a 152% lift in conversion against videos that did not clearly state the next step. For e-commerce, a regional version of the post says on-screen text that clearly shows an offer or call to action saw an 80% lift, and that these results were validated globally except in Brazil, Japan and North America. — [TikTok for Business blog](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-US/blog/creative-that-drives-conversions)
- **[Official, platform research, May 2023, Australian retail]** Pairing a call to action with a price point: +87% conversion lift. — [TikTok Works one-pager](https://www.tiktok.com/business/library/TikTok_Works_Performance_Creative_1_Pager.pdf)
- **[Official, platform data]** Ads using a call-to-action display card see 45% stronger recall (a recall metric, not conversion). — [TikTok Creative Center](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/creativecenter/insight)
- **[Vendor with platform, dated: 2020]** A call to action in the opening frame led to a 44% lift in conversion rate against showing it later. — [TikTok for Business blog on VidMob](https://www.tiktok.com/business/es/blog/vidmobs-data-driven-insights-to-supercharge-ad-creative-on-tiktok)
- **[Small sample, vendor, date not confirmed]** Fanpage Karma, 540 Reels: Reels with a call to action performed worse overall than Reels without one. Only a prompt to comment was associated with higher interactions and higher reach. Save and share prompts were not broken out. — [Fanpage Karma](https://blog.fanpagekarma.com/?p=22627)
- **[Large sample, vendor, sample size not captured]** Socialinsider: Reels have a share rate of 0.10%, carousels 0.08%, images 0.07%. The page does not say whether posts used share prompts. — [Socialinsider engagement report](https://www.socialinsider.io/social-media-benchmarks/instagram-engagement-report)

**Comment a keyword to receive something by direct message**
- **[Vendor's own figures]** ManyChat puts automated-message conversion at 12–20% ("qualified leads convert from 12-20%") and reports a 10–30% engagement uplift for users of its tool. — [ManyChat](https://manychat.com/blog/conversion-rates-by-marketing-channel/); [HypeAuditor](https://hypeauditor.com/manychat/)
- **[Vendor, mid-size sample, secondary]** Creator Lane: 52,633 delivered out of 57,052 matched attempts, a 92.25% delivery rate. It declined to publish a click-through rate because tracking covered only 28.85% of messages. Planoly's conclusion: there is no standard benchmark because vendors define conversion differently; compare against your own link-in-bio results. — [Planoly](https://www.planoly.com/blog/do-automated-instagram-dms-work) (Planoly's article is the probable carrier of the Creator Lane figures; search did not confirm the page)
- **[Lore]** "80–100% open rates" and "20–28% click-through" for automated Instagram messages: unsourced. One creator reports click-through falling from over 70% to under 20%. — [Unkoa](https://www.unkoa.com/instagram-dm-automation-vs-email-in-2025-why-manychat-delivers-90-open-rates-and-60-reply-rates/); [Nowbam](https://nowbam.com/?p=165855)
- **[Practitioner]** A keyword-for-message prompt is legitimate when viewers understand what they will receive; avoid stacking several calls to action in one post. — [Aubrium](https://aubrium.com/blog/comment-x-and-ill-dm-you); [GrowthViral](https://growthviral.co/instagram-growth-hub/engagement/how-to-get-more-comments-on-instagram/) (search did not say which of the two guides each point is from)

**Where a call to action can physically go**
- **[Official]** YouTube: links in Shorts comments and Shorts descriptions are not clickable (rolled out from 2023-08-31, to curb spam). Channel profile links and long-form descriptions and comments remain clickable, per a vendor help page. — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13748639); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/08/10/youtube-is-disabling-links-on-shorts-to-cut-down-on-spam); [Outfy](https://www.outfy.com/help/docs/why-are-some-links-not-clickable-on-youtube/)
- **[Official, secondary, 2023]** YouTube added one "related video" link, set in YouTube Studio, from a Short to another video on the same channel. Sources disagree on its current status. — [Search Engine Journal](https://searchenginejournal.com/youtube-adds-shorts-links-limits-links-elsewhere/495463)
- **[Single claim]** Direct commands suit how-to content; curiosity-style prompts suit entertainment and behind-the-scenes content. Opinion, no dataset. — [AutomateEd](https://automateed.com/call-to-action-ideas-for-reels)

### Inferences
- Call-to-action types against what the rules allow and what evidence exists. Rule details and sources are in question 6.

| Type | Usual place (convention, not measured) | Rule position | Evidence of effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| Follow or subscribe | Spoken at the end; caption | YouTube explicitly allows asking. Instagram and TikTok object only to manipulation | None found |
| Comment a keyword, get a message | Spoken and on screen near the end; caption; pinned comment | Instagram names it as an example of what it will not recommend when used to inflate reach, and says it is fine for real lead generation | Vendor figures only (12–20% conversion) |
| Save | On-screen text at the end; caption | No rule names saves | None found; saves are not in Instagram's top three signals |
| Share or send to a friend | Spoken or on screen at the end | Explicit asks for shares fall inside Instagram's June 2024 wording | Sends are a top ranking signal; no data on whether asking raises them |
| Link in bio, lead magnet | Spoken at the end; caption | Not clickable from Shorts descriptions or comments | None found |
| Visit or book | On-screen text, early and again at the end | No restriction found | TikTok ad data: text call to action and price both lift conversion |

- The paid data says: put the call to action on screen as text, make the next step explicit, and do not hold it for the last frame. The organic data (thin) says a generic bolt-on ask can cost reach. Both are consistent with one specific call to action tied to the content, not a list of asks.
- Pinned-comment links cannot work on Shorts because comment links are not clickable there. On Shorts the usable routes are the related-video link, the channel profile, and a spoken instruction.
- Illustrative wording, written by me (not sourced, not tested):
  - Send: "Send this to the person who keeps saying we'll fix the website later."
  - Keyword for a message: "Want the checklist? Comment CHECKLIST and I'll message it to you." (says what the viewer receives)
  - Save: "Save this for your next quote."
  - Book: "Four slots left in March. The booking link is on our profile."
  - Shorts: "The full walkthrough is linked on this Short."
- The only sourced wording in this section is Instagram's description of what it treats as bait: asking people "to comment with a specific word, number, or emoji".

### Gaps
- No data found on call-to-action placement (end, middle, caption, pinned comment) for organic short video.
- No measurement of whether "save this" or "send this" prompts raise saves or sends.
- No independent click-through or conversion benchmark for comment-to-message flows.
- No link-in-bio click-through data from a credible sample.
- Sourced word-for-word call-to-action examples from top videos were not collected.
- Planned search that did not run: the details and date of the Fanpage Karma 540-Reel study.
- Unverified leads from my background knowledge: TikTok business accounts have native automatic messages (welcome message, keyword replies), and ManyChat added TikTok automation around 2024–2025. TikTok's link in bio depends on account type or follower count.

## 6. Platform rules and ranking effects for calls to action

### Takeaway
Instagram's position since June 2024: content that explicitly asks for engagement to inflate reach will not be recommended, and the named example is asking for a specific word, number or emoji in the comments; using comments and messages to generate leads or sales is explicitly not engagement bait. Automated replies are permitted through Meta's messaging interface under fixed limits: one private reply per comment, within 7 days, and nothing further until the person responds. YouTube explicitly allows asking for likes, comments and subscriptions; TikTok bars content that tricks or manipulates people into engaging. I found no on-record statement from Adam Mosseri about "comment X to get Y" posts specifically.

### Cited Findings

**Meta and Instagram: engagement bait**
- **[Official, relayed by search as a paraphrase]** Meta's content distribution guideline describes engagement bait as posts that explicitly ask for votes, shares, comments, tags, likes or other reactions for purposes other than a specific call to action. Exceptions include finding missing people or property, raising money, sharing a petition, showing support for an issue, and urgent information about disasters. Meta lowers the distribution of such content. — [Meta Transparency Center](https://transparency.meta.com/features/approach-to-ranking/content-distribution-guidelines/engagement-bait/); [Meta: lowering distribution](https://transparency.meta.com/enforcement/taking-action/lowering-distribution-of-problematic-content/)
- **[Official, dated: December 2017]** Facebook's original announcement: posts that prompt people to interact through likes, shares, comments, tags or votes are shown less, and Pages that do it systematically and repeatedly get page-level demotion. Reported subtypes: vote baiting, react baiting, share baiting, tag baiting, comment baiting. Search reports later extensions to comments and to bait spoken in a video's audio. — [Meta Newsroom](https://about.fb.com/news/2017/12/news-feed-fyi-fighting-engagement-bait-on-facebook/); [Snopes](https://www.snopes.com/2017/12/18/facebook-downrank-engagement-bait-spammers/)
- **[Official]** Instagram's Recommendations Guidelines list engagement bait and clickbait among content it may keep out of recommendations such as Explore and Reels. — [Instagram Help Center](https://help.instagram.com/313829416281232)
- **[Official, secondary, June 2024]** Instagram's creators account: "Don't try to hack engagement, and here's what we mean by that: If we believe your content explicitly asks for engagement through shares, comments, tags, or other actions, then we won't recommend it. An example of this is asking people to comment with a specific word, number, or emoji." Instagram posted the advice, deleted it, then restored it with this wording. — [Matt Navarra on Threads](https://www.threads.com/@mattnavarra/post/C73yPjiArtZ); [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-says-using-certain-ctas-can-impact-post-reach/717732/)
- **[Official, secondary, June 2024]** Instagram's clarification: "So long as [creators are] actually engaging with their audience, it's not engagement bait. What is engagement bait is if the end goal is to simply maximize the quantity of engagements as a way to 'hack' reach rather than connect with their audience." And: earnestly engaging with your audience in the comments and messages "to connect personally, generate leads, or drive conversions is not considered to be engagement bait." Social Media Today noted it is unclear how automated systems tell the two apart, and that tools such as ManyChat rely on single-word triggers. — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-clarifies-advice-single-word-ctas-longer-reels/718151/)
- **[Official]** Instagram's monetisation policies: content that incentivises people to click a link or respond to a post through likes or comments cannot be monetised. — [Instagram Help Center](https://help.instagram.com/2635536099905516)
- **[Practitioner observation]** Meta's written engagement-bait definition is written for Facebook; one reviewer found no Instagram ranking document that cites it. — [Aubrium](https://aubrium.com/blog/engagement-bait-policy)

**Instagram: rules for automated direct messages**
- **[Official]** Private replies: "You can send this private reply within 7 days of the creation time of the comment, excepting Instagram Live for which you can only send a private reply during the live broadcast." One private reply per comment. "Only when the Instagram user responds to the private message can you continue the conversation within the 24-hour messaging window." — [Meta for Developers](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/instagram-messaging/features/private-replies)
- **[Tool documentation]** ManyChat: "The 24-hour window will only open after the user interacts with your message — for example, by replying or clicking a regular button or quick reply." "Only then will they be fully opted in and added to your Manychat contact list." ManyChat blocks scheduled messages to contacts whose window has closed. — [ManyChat Help Center](https://help.manychat.com/hc/en-us/articles/14281316989724); [ManyChat blog](https://manychat.com/blog/instagram-dm-automation-rules/)
- **[Tool vendors]** Messages inside the 24-hour window may contain promotional content. Private replies to comments are limited to 750 calls per hour per professional account (Planoly). The often-quoted "200 messages per hour" limit appears nowhere in Meta's documentation (SocialPilot). One vendor says the comment trigger fires only on a user's first comment under a post. — [Planoly](https://www.planoly.com/blog/instagram-24-hour-messaging-window-explained); [SocialPilot](https://www.socialpilot.co/strategy/instagram-comment-to-dm-automation); [SuperProfile](https://superprofile.bio/auto-dm/use-cases/comment-to-dm.md) (probable page for the first-comment point; search named only "one vendor")
- **[Tool vendor, self-interested]** ManyChat says comment automation is allowed when it runs through Meta's official interface; a third party notes ManyChat is a Meta Business Partner and that Meta, not the tool, decides restrictions. — [ManyChat](https://manychat.com/blog/comments-automation-instagram/); [Kanal](https://getkanal.com/blog/manychat-instagram)

**YouTube**
- **[Official]** "You're allowed to encourage viewers to subscribe, hit the like button, share or leave a comment." Prohibited: subscribing in exchange for a subscription, and selling views, likes, comments or other metrics. Violations lead to removal and, for repeat or severe abuse, channel termination. — [YouTube fake engagement policy](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/3399767?hl=en); [YouTube spam policy](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2801973)
- **[Official]** Links in Shorts comments and descriptions are not clickable; YouTube said it had no plans to make other links unclickable. — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13748639); [YouTube links update](https://yt.be/help/links)

**TikTok**
- **[Official]** Integrity and Authenticity guideline: content is ineligible for the For You feed if it seeks to trick or manipulate others to increase gifts, followers, likes, views or other engagement metrics. Trading services that artificially increase engagement is prohibited. — [TikTok Community Guidelines](https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/integrity-authenticity/)
- **[Secondary, August 2025]** TikTok's 2025 guideline update no longer has one long list of content ineligible for the For You feed; the details are spread across sections. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/08/15/tiktoks-new-guidelines-add-subtle-changes-for-live-creators-ai-content-and-more/)
- **[Single claim, unverified against TikTok's text]** A third-party site says "like-for-like" and fake incentives count as engagement bait under this policy, and that TikTok supports genuine questions and invitations to discuss. — [Audit Socials](https://www.auditsocials.com/platforms/tiktok-community-guidelines)

### Inferences
- A compliant comment-to-message flow on Instagram, read off the cited rules: the video says what the viewer will receive; the tool sends one private reply per comment within 7 days; that reply invites a tap or a reply; further messages go only to people who responded, inside 24 hours of their last interaction.
- The risk is not the automation but the ask. Instagram's stated test is intent (inflating reach against serving the audience), and its own systems have to guess intent from the wording. A bare "comment YES" is closer to the named bad example than "comment GUIDE and I'll send you the guide".
- "Share" appears in Instagram's June 2024 list of explicit asks. I found no official exemption for "send this to a friend" prompts, even though sends are the signal Instagram says it values most. Treat spoken or on-screen share prompts as carrying the same uncertainty as comment prompts.
- Platform differences in one line: YouTube permits direct asks; Instagram permits them for real interaction and penalises them as a reach tactic; TikTok penalises trickery and says little about plain asks.

### Gaps

Three searches returned only the June 2024 creators-account statements and vendor claims. A planned search of his 2025 podcast interviews did not run.
- I found no evidence that Instagram has launched a native comment-to-message feature in 2026; results were vendor marketing.
- I could not open Meta's engagement-bait page or the private-replies documentation, so the wording above is as relayed by search. The dates search gave for the comment and audio extensions (November 2018 and January 2019) are unconfirmed.
- TikTok's and YouTube's rules for automated messages were not researched (planned search did not run). Title-only leads: TikTok Messaging Ads policies (https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/tiktok-messaging-ads-policies?lang=en).
- 2026 Instagram policy changes surfaced by title only and not read: reposted-content rules (https://petapixel.com/2026-04-30/new-instagram-policies-target-reposted-content/); Instagram's recommendation eligibility pages (https://creators.instagram.com/recommendations-and-originality).
- Whether the related-video link on Shorts still works in 2026 is unresolved: sources conflict.

## 7. Shares ("sends per reach") and saves as ranking signals, and what that means for the call to action

### Takeaway
Instagram's head has said on record that the top three ranking signals are watch time, likes and sends, that sends matter slightly more for reaching non-followers, and that rates per reach matter more than counts. Saves are not in that top three. So for growth on Instagram, the call to action worth a slot is the one that produces a send; for TikTok and YouTube I found no official statement that shares carry special weight.

### Cited Findings
- **[Official statement, secondary, January 2025]** Mosseri: "The top three signals that matter most for ranking are watch time, likes and sends." "Likes are slightly more important for connected content, and sends are slightly more important for unconnected content." Track average watch time, likes per reach and sends per reach. — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-shares-algorithm-insights-2025/738034/)
- **[Official statement, secondary]** Mosseri on sends per reach: one of the most important signals used in ranking; of the people who saw a post, "how many of them sent it to a friend in a DM?" — [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/instagram-algorithm-shift-why-sends-matter-more-than-ever/521389/)
- **[Official statement, secondary, question-and-answer video]** Mosseri: "More important than watch time or like and comment counts is send rates, [and] generally, I think the rate is more important than the count." Sends per reach "correlate more, in my experience, with overall reach than anything else". — [t3n](https://t3n.de/news/?p=1671110)
- **[Secondary]** Saves are not among the three signals Mosseri names. One outlet says saves "can also play a role". Buffer notes that Instagram says which signals matter most but not how much each weighs, so claimed multipliers deserve suspicion. — [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources//instagram-algorithms/); [OnlineMarketing.de](https://onlinemarketing.de/social-media-marketing/warum-instagram-weiterhin-likes-fokussiert)
- **[Large sample, vendor]** Reels are shared more than other formats: share rate 0.10% against 0.08% for carousels and 0.07% for images. — [Socialinsider engagement report](https://www.socialinsider.io/social-media-benchmarks/instagram-engagement-report)
- **[Official]** TikTok: recommendations come from a prediction of how likely you are to interact with each video; inputs are user interactions, video information, and device and account settings. Search relays that interactions, including time spent watching, are weighted more heavily than other factors. TikTok does not rank the interactions against each other. — [TikTok transparency](https://www.tiktok.com/transparency/recommendation-system/?lang=hi); [TikTok Newsroom](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/en-us/how-tiktok-recommends-videos-for-you/)
- **[Practitioner, unofficial]** Third-party guides list watch time, completion rate, replays and shares as TikTok's strongest signals. This is their reading, not TikTok's. — [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources//tiktok-algorithm/)
- **[Official by absence]** I found no YouTube source saying shares are specially weighted for Shorts. Guides list likes, comments and shares together as engagement. — [Hootsuite](https://blog.hootsuite.com/youtube-shorts-algorithm/)
- **[Lore]** "Save plus share rate explains 27% of reach variance; save rate predicts 7-day reach at r = 0.71 (n = 1,200)". Telegram post, no method. — [Telegram post](https://t.me/s/ReachLabReports/13)

### Inferences
- Comments are not one of Instagram's three named signals. A comment-keyword call to action is therefore a lead-capture device, not a reach device, and should be judged on leads.
- If the aim is new viewers on Instagram, design for the send: content a viewer would forward to a specific person (a shared problem, a useful checklist, something that says "this is you"). Whether asking for the send helps is unmeasured, and the ask itself sits in a grey area (question 6).
- If the aim is existing followers, likes count slightly more than sends, so the content matters more than any share prompt.
- "Save this" has the weakest case of the engagement prompts: saves are not a named top signal and no data shows the prompt works. It still fits reference content, where a save is the natural action.
- With a reels share rate around 0.10%, a small account needs very few sends per reel to be above average. That is a reason to track sends per reach on each reel instead of chasing counts.

### Gaps
- Mosseri's answer to "Why do you prioritise sends over saves in ranking?" (an August 2024 question listed in a thesis annex) was not retrieved.
- No send-rate or save-rate benchmarks by account size for Reels were found. One write-up gives save rates of 0.04% for Reels, 0.05% for carousels and 0.02% for images without naming the study.
- No official weighting of shares on TikTok or YouTube Shorts.
- Planned search that did not run: Buffer's 2026 study of 52M+ posts.
- Unverified lead from my background knowledge: Instagram's official post "Instagram Ranking Explained" (2023, https://about.instagram.com/blog/announcements/instagram-ranking-explained) lists, for Reels, predictions of how likely a viewer is to reshare, watch to the end, like, and visit the audio page. Search returned the page by title only.

## 8. Service businesses, local businesses and B2B compared with entertainment creators

### Takeaway
I found demand-side data (people do discover local businesses through short video, and most local competitors are not active) but almost nothing measured on which hooks or calls to action produce bookings or leads for service, local or B2B accounts. The rules favour these accounts: Instagram explicitly says using comments and messages to generate leads is not engagement bait. Most hook and call-to-action benchmarks come from creators or from e-commerce ads and do not transfer cleanly.

### Cited Findings
- **[Large sample, platform-commissioned, 2026]** TikTok's 2026 US Economic Impact Report: 65% of TikTok users surveyed have discovered a local business, attraction or event through the platform; 70% among ages 18–24. — [eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/tiktok-drives-local-discovery-small-businesses-seek-new-customers)
- **[Survey, vendor, 2026]** SOCi's 2026 Local Discovery Index (more than 1,000 US consumers): use of social media to discover local businesses rose from 17% in 2024 to 55% in 2026; 43% use social to preview what a place is like before visiting. — [MarTech](https://martech.org/ai-usage-surges-6x-for-local-search-fragmenting-the-digital-ecosystem/) (probable source; search did not confirm the page)
- **[Survey, vendor, 2026]** Adobe with SurveyMonkey: 49% of consumers surveyed used TikTok as a search engine in 2026. — [Adobe Express](https://www.adobe.com/express/learn/blog/using-tiktok-as-a-search-engine)
- **[Survey, 2026]** BrightLocal's 2026 Small Business Marketing Report (1,002 US owners): 31% of small business owners keep an active presence on TikTok. — [eMarketer chart](https://www.emarketer.com/chart/c/375802/just-31-of-small-business-owners-maintain-active-presence-on-tiktok-375802)
- **[Official, secondary, June 2024]** Instagram: engaging in comments and messages "to connect personally, generate leads, or drive conversions is not considered to be engagement bait." — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-clarifies-advice-single-word-ctas-longer-reels/718151/)
- **[Large sample, vendor]** For typical accounts, opening on the product or outcome ranks first on TikTok and opening on expertise or credibility ranks first on YouTube. Both are natural for a service business. — [OpusClip TikTok hooks](https://www.opus.pro/research/best-video-hooks-tiktok); [OpusClip YouTube hooks](https://www.opus.pro/research/best-video-hooks-youtube)
- **[Large sample, vendor]** Small accounts get proportionally more reach per reel: 9.78% reach rate at 1–5K followers against 5.00% at 100K–1M. — [Influencer Marketing Hub](https://influencermarketinghub.com/instagram-reels-stats/)
- **[Platform-sourced, secondary; search did not map each figure to a page]** LinkedIn: video consumption up 36% year over year; short-form video creation growing at twice the rate of other formats; completions up 34%; short, vertical "real talk" videos showed 103% higher dwell time. — [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/linkedin-study-b2b-video-ads-engagement-lift-458873); [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/linkedin-creative-labs-reveals-what-makes-b2b-video-content-successful/); [Bold Content](https://boldcontentvideo.com/2026/02/26/what-linkedins-2025-research-tells-us-about-video/)
- **[Single claims, B2B blogs, unverified]** Lead-generation forms on video ads convert at 6–10%. A 3-second view rate of 15–25% of impressions is "average" (platform not stated). Founder or executive talking heads with little editing outperform polished brand video. Suggested structure: problem, solution, call to action. — [Sprout Social](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/linkedin-video/); [Whitehat](https://whitehat-seo.co.uk/blog/video-in-business-benchmarks); [Sotros](https://sotrosinfotech.com/blog/b2b-video-marketing-linkedin-youtube-strategy-benchmarks-2026/) (search did not map each figure to a page)
- **[Scope note]** Billo's hook-rate industries (pet supplies, toys, apparel, health and beauty) and Motion's leading tactics (sale, price, urgency) are e-commerce. Neither dataset covers services. — [Billo](https://billo.app/blog/hook-rate-to-hold-rate); [Motion](https://motionapp.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/top-hook-tactics)

### Inferences
- The goal differs. A creator needs reach from strangers anywhere; a local or service business needs a few of the right people nearby to act. Reach benchmarks and viral-tier lengths are the wrong yardstick; leads, messages and bookings per video are the right one.
- Hooks that fit: name the audience or place (label and if-then call-outs), then show the finished result or the proof in the first frame. This matches the measured winners for typical accounts and tells the wrong viewers to leave, which is acceptable when the market is local.
- Calls to action that fit: comment a keyword for a specific item (permitted for lead generation and the only route with any conversion figures), or a direct book or visit instruction. A follow prompt is worth less to a business with a bounded market.
- For B2B, the available signals point to short, plain, person-to-camera vertical video with a problem-solution shape and a lead form or message as the next step. Everything here is platform marketing or blog claims.
- Only 31% of small business owners are active on TikTok while most users say they find local businesses there. Low competition is a better argument for a service business than any hook benchmark.

### Gaps
- No data found on which hooks or calls to action drive bookings, enquiries or qualified leads for service, local or B2B accounts.
- No benchmark for skip rate, hook rate or send rate specific to service or local accounts.
- The LinkedIn figures reached me second-hand and unmapped; the planned search for LinkedIn's own research did not run.
- Location features (location tags, local feeds, search ranking for local queries) were not researched.
- Unverified lead from my background knowledge: since July 2025, public posts from Instagram professional accounts can appear in Google results, which would make the spoken and written keywords in a hook and caption matter for local search.
