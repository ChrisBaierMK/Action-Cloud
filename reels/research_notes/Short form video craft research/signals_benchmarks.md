## 1. Official statements from 2024–2026: Instagram, TikTok, YouTube Shorts

### Takeaway

All three platforms describe the same family of signals (did the viewer stay instead of skipping, how long they watched, did they pass it on), and none publishes weights. Instagram is the most explicit (Mosseri, January 2025: watch time, likes per reach, sends per reach). YouTube changed what a Shorts "view" means on 31 March 2025 and kept "engaged views" for payment. TikTok's own text is the least specific, so the popular "completion and rewatch rank highest" hierarchy rests on third-party guides.

### Cited Findings

#### Instagram

- **Top three signals, January 2025.** Adam Mosseri: "The top three signals that matter most for ranking are watch time, likes and sends." He told creators to track average watch time, likes per reach and sends per reach in Insights. Likes per reach is tied to reaching followers, sends per reach to reaching non-followers. — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-shares-algorithm-insights-2025/738034/), dated 22 Jan 2025 by secondary citations; [Buffer, 2026 guide](https://buffer.com/resources/instagram-algorithms/). **[A-press]**
- **Connected versus unconnected reach.** The wording repeated across the 2026 guides returned by search is "Likes are slightly more important for connected content, and sends are slightly more important for unconnected content," attributed to the same January 2025 Mosseri video. The guides returned include [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/instagram-algorithms/), [PostEverywhere](https://posteverywhere.ai/blog/how-the-instagram-algorithm-works) and [Hanami Social](https://hanamisocial.com/en/blog/instagram-reels-algorithm-2026/); the summary did not say which page carries the exact quote. **[A-press, relayed by C]**
- **Sends per reach, emphasized since 2024.** Mosseri said sends per reach correlates with overall reach more than other metrics, and that the rate matters more than the raw count. — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-chief-says-post-share-rates-are-now-a-key-driver-of-reach/716540/), 2024; [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/instagram-algorithm-shift-why-sends-matter-more-than-ever/521389/), 2024; [Uppbeat](https://uppbeat.io/blog/creator-news/whats-working-on-instagram-right-now-carousels-and-sends-says-adam-mosseri). Exact dates not shown in results. **[A-press]**
- **No weights are published.** Secondary guides note that Instagram names the signals that matter most but not how much each one weighs; Mosseri's own qualifier is "slightly more important." — e.g. [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/instagram-algorithms/), 2026. **[C]**
- **Originality and recommendation eligibility, 30 April 2024.** Instagram said: "historically because of how we've ranked content, creators with large followings and aggregators of reposted content have gotten more reach in recommendations than smaller, original content creators." It announced four changes: a new ranking input to give smaller creators more distribution, replacing reposts with the original in recommendations, labelling reposted content, and removing aggregators from recommendations. A repost is swapped for the original only when the original is relatively new; content that has been significantly changed is not replaced; the original creator is notified. — [Instagram Creators](https://creators.instagram.com/recommendations-and-originality), 30 Apr 2024 **[A]**; [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/04/30/instagram-is-updating-its-ranking-systems-to-surface-more-content-from-smaller-original-creators), 30 Apr 2024 **[A-press]**
- **Originality, expanded 30 April 2026.** The protections now cover photos and carousels: aggregators who primarily re-upload others' work in photos and carousel posts are no longer eligible for recommendations anywhere on Instagram. Eligibility is calculated on a rolling basis and standing is shown in Account Status; one outlet describes the assessment as covering what an account publishes over the course of a month, not single posts. Creators can remove unoriginal content or appeal. Low-effort edits such as adding a watermark or changing a video's speed do not count as an original contribution, and neither does a screenshot of someone else's post with the username visible. Reach to existing followers is not limited. Instagram also said 75% of recommendations in the US now come from original posts. — [Instagram Creators](https://creators.instagram.com/blog/rewarding-original-creators-on-instagram), 30 Apr 2026 **[A]**; [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/30/instagram-restricts-reach-of-content-aggregators-in-new-crackdown/), [Tubefilter](https://tubefilter.com/2026/04/30/instagram-removes-algorithm-recommendations-repost-content-aggregator), [PetaPixel](https://petapixel.com/2026/04/30/new-instagram-policies-target-reposted-content/), all 30 Apr 2026 **[A-press]**
- **Earlier originality history.** Mosseri first announced ranking changes to favour original content on 20 April 2022. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/04/20/instagram-ranking-system-recommend-original-content/), 20 Apr 2022. **[A-press]**, old.
- **What a "view" is, August 2024.** Instagram made "views" the primary metric across Reels, photos, carousels and Stories; "Reel Plays" was relabelled Views. Meta's creator page says that for Reels and videos the way views are calculated does not change, and "we're removing the separate replays metric." A Reel view needs no minimum watch time (one tool's summary of Meta's definition says at least 1 millisecond of playback). The API followed in April 2025, when Plays and Impressions were replaced by Views. — [Meta for Creators](https://creators.facebook.com/introducing-views-to-simplify-content), Aug 2024 **[A]**; [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2024/08/07/instagram-views-reels-posts-multi-format-adam-mosseri/amp/), 7 Aug 2024 **[A-press]**; [Sked Social help](https://help.skedsocial.com/updates-to-instagram-metrics-april-2025), Apr 2025 **[C]**; [Brandwatch help](https://social-media-management-help.brandwatch.com/en/articles/12767947) **[C]**
- **The first three seconds: view rate, then skip rate.** In late January 2025 Instagram added "View Rate," which "lets creators see what percentage of their followers and non-followers continue to watch their video after the first three seconds." Around August 2025 it added a retention chart and "skip rate" (the share of viewers who leave within the first three seconds) and said skip rate would replace view rate. A later update put share-rate and skip-rate percentages on the main Insights overview. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/01/29/instagram-gives-creators-more-insight-into-their-reels-performance), 29 Jan 2025; [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-adds-retention-insights-reels/758464/), c. Aug 2025; [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-improves-insights-ui-adds-new-metrics/818504/), 2026 (date inferred, not shown). **[A-press]**; sources disagree on some dates.
- **API, 3 December 2025.** Meta added five new metrics to the Instagram Insights endpoint and updated the views metric. — [Meta for Developers](https://developers.facebook.com/blog/post/2025/12/03/instragram-api-updates/), 3 Dec 2025. **[A]**; whether skip rate is exposed was not confirmed.
- **Trial reels, 10 December 2024.** A trial reel is shown to non-followers first: followers do not see it in Feed or the Reels tab, and it does not appear on the profile grid or Reels tab unless the creator later shares it with everyone. — [Instagram Creators](https://creators.instagram.com/blog/instagram-trial-reels) and [Meta Newsroom](https://about.fb.com/news/2024/12/trial-reels-try-content-non-followers-first/), Dec 2024 **[A]**; [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/12/10/instagram-rolls-out-trial-reels-that-arent-shown-to-a-creators-followers), 10 Dec 2024 **[A-press]**
- **Trial reels, details from third parties.** Metrics become useful about 24 hours after posting; a trial that performs well can be shared to followers automatically after 72 hours; followers may still meet a trial reel through a DM or an audio, location or filter page. Eligibility thresholds vary by source and over time (one guide: 200 followers for professional accounts, 1,000 for personal). Access widened in July 2025. — third-party guides returned by search, among them [Sprout Social](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/instagram-trial-reels/) and [Soundstripe](https://www.soundstripe.com/blogs/what-are-instagram-trial-reels) **[C]**; [BetaNews](https://betanews.com/2025/07/14/instagram-opens-up-trial-reels-feature-to-more-creators/), 14 Jul 2025 **[A-press]**
- **Length limits, January 2025.** Mosseri announced that the Reels limit doubled from 90 seconds to 3 minutes, citing creator feedback. Above 3 minutes Instagram shows the alert "Reels over 3 minutes won't be recommended to new audiences": followers can still see the reel, but it is unlikely to appear in the Reels tab, on Explore or in non-followers' feeds. Buffer's table lists 20 minutes as the maximum upload length; no other source in the results confirms that figure. — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-officially-expands-reels-length-to-3-minutes/737766/), Jan 2025 **[A-press]**; [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/instagram-reels-length), 2026 **[C]**
- **Superseded length advice.** In 2024 Instagram showed creators a slide saying "Reels that are longer than 90 seconds can hurt distribution." — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-longer-reels-hurt-performance/715867/), 2024. **[A-press]**; replaced by the January 2025 change.

#### TikTok

- **Factor list, 2020 and still the base text.** TikTok's newsroom lists the For You inputs as likes, shares, accounts followed, comments and content the user creates, starting from the interests a new user states and adjusting for "not interested" feedback. It says it avoids showing two videos in a row with the same sound or creator and does not recommend duplicated content, content already seen, or spam. — [TikTok Newsroom](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/en-us/how-tiktok-recommends-videos-for-you/), 18 Jun 2020. **[A]**, old.
- **Device, language and location settings get lower weight** than other factors. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/06/18/tiktok-explains-how-the-recommendation-system-behind-its-for-you-feed-works), 18 Jun 2020. **[A-press]**, old.
- **Weights are personal, 2023.** A November 2023 newsroom post (a regional edition, Malaysia) says the factors are weighted by their value to each user. — [TikTok Newsroom](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/how-tik-tok-recommends-video-for-you?lang=en-MY), 1 Nov 2023. **[A]**
- **How ranking works.** The transparency page says several machine-learning models select candidate videos, predict how the user will respond and rank them by prediction score; the top-ranked videos must also pass a final similarity check. The page sends readers to the Help Center for "up-to-date information on how TikTok recommends content." — [TikTok Transparency Center](https://www.tiktok.com/transparency/recommendation-system/), undated in results. **[A]**
- **Follower count.** Sprout Social reports that TikTok does not directly consider follower count or previous high-performing videos. — [Sprout Social](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/tiktok-algorithm/). **[C]** relaying an official statement.
- **Completion and rewatch.** The official pages found mention only general signals from viewing behavior and give no weighting or completion rule. The ranked hierarchy comes from guides: Hootsuite puts watch time, completion rate, replays and shares first, then comments and saves, then likes, with device, language and location last, and adds that the exact weighting is proprietary and likely changes. Buffer's 2026 guide says the weighting of watch time and engagement is not publicly confirmed. — [Hootsuite](https://blog.hootsuite.com/tiktok-algorithm/), [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/tiktok-algorithm/), 2026. **[C]**
- **Creator Rewards Program, eligibility.** Creators must be at least 18, have at least 10,000 followers and at least 100,000 views in the last 30 days, and hold a personal account in good standing in a supported region. — [TikTok Newsroom](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/introducing-the-new-creator-rewards-program), 18 Mar 2024. **[A]**
- **Creator Rewards, content rules (third-party).** Guides agree that only original videos longer than one minute earn. Qualified views are described as unique views from the For You feed; payment starts at 1,000 qualified views. The minimum watch time for a qualified view conflicts: 5 seconds in one guide, 30 seconds in another, and a third says views from Search count when watched at least 30 seconds, citing TikTok's Creator Academy. Ads and sponsored content are excluded; paid and disliked views do not count. — [Metricool](https://metricool.com/tiktok-creator-next/), [Manychat](https://manychat.com/blog/tiktok-creator-rewards-program/), [Mubert](https://mubert.com/blog/?p=5211), [Toptal](https://www.toptal.com/creator/post/how-to-join-the-tiktok-creator-rewards-program), 2025–2026. **[C]**, conflicting on the watch-time threshold.
- **Creator Rewards and search.** Coverage of the launch headlined the program as paying "creators who make trending search content," and TikTok paired it with a Creator Search Insights tool. — [Yahoo News](https://sg.news.yahoo.com/tiktok-launches-creator-rewards-program-165036440.html), Mar 2024; [Hootsuite](https://blog.hootsuite.com/social-media-updates/tiktok/creator-rewards-program-search-insights-tool/). **[A-press]**
- **Creator Rewards, pay.** Reported rates run from about $0.40 to $1.00 per 1,000 qualified views, or $0.36 to $1.04 in another roundup; TikTok publishes no fixed rate. "Additional Rewards," formalized in 2025, sit on top and are tied to engagement and perceived content quality. — [Soundstripe](https://www.soundstripe.com/blogs/how-much-does-tiktok-pay-per-view), 2026; [Mubert](https://mubert.com/blog/?p=5211); [Influencer Marketing Hub](https://influencermarketinghub.com/?p=222403). **[C]**
- **For You eligibility.** Community Guidelines: content is ineligible for the For You feed if it is "unoriginal or reproduced content that does not have any new or creative changes." Revised guidelines took effect 13 September 2025, and the For You Eligibility Standards section no longer carries a long list of ineligible content types. — [TikTok Community Guidelines](https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/integrity-authenticity/), released 17 Apr 2024 **[A]**; [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/08/15/tiktoks-new-guidelines-add-subtle-changes-for-live-creators-ai-content-and-more/), 15 Aug 2025 **[A-press]**
- **United States joint venture, 22 January 2026.** TikTok formed TikTok USDS Joint Venture LLC. The US recommendation algorithm is licensed from ByteDance and retrained on US user data, with Oracle securing the data and the retrained model. Forrester's Kelsey Chickering: what dominates feeds "will feel distinctly American"; "Global content will still appear, but its ranking will change." — [Forrester](https://www.forrester.com/blogs/tiktok-seals-the-deal-with-new-us-joint-venture), Jan 2026 **[C]**; [Times of Israel](https://www.timesofisrael.com/tiktok-finalizes-deal-to-create-us-entity-and-retrain-algorithm-avoiding-ban/), Jan 2026 **[A-press]**; [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/computing/tiktok/the-deal-to-keep-tiktok-in-the-us-is-now-finalized-here-are-5-things-you-need-to-know) **[C]**

#### YouTube Shorts

- **How views are counted, from 31 March 2025.** A view is counted "each time a Short starts to play or replay, no minimum watch time required." YouTube said views "may be higher" from then on. Coverage of the announcement is dated 26 March 2025. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/26/youtube-is-changing-how-youtube-shorts-views-are-counted), [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2025/03/26/youtube-shorts-views-counting-stats/), [9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2025/03/26/youtube-shorts-views-metric/), all 26 Mar 2025. **[A-press]**, consistent across outlets.
- **Engaged views.** The previous metric survives as "engaged views" in YouTube Analytics, in Advanced Mode. YouTube advises using engaged views to compare content published before and after 31 March 2025. Partner Program eligibility and earnings stay based on engaged views: "YPP earnings will still be based on 'engaged Shorts views' and 'engaged watch hours'." — [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-changes-shorts-view-counts-no-change-to-monetization/543005/), Mar 2025 **[A-press]**; [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12220281) **[A]**
- **Why the change.** eMarketer framed it as matching TikTok and Instagram, which already count a view at playback start. Digiday reported that brands welcomed it while some creators expected revenue per thousand views to fall as view counts rise. — [eMarketer](https://emarketer.com/content/youtube-shorts-changes-view-count-rules-match-tiktok--instagram), Mar 2025; [Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/youtube-shorts-view-count-update-wins-over-brands-but-creators-arent-sold/), 2025. **[C]**
- **Viewed versus swiped away.** YouTube Help defines it as "The percentage of times viewers viewed your Shorts versus swiped away." — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12220281), undated. **[A]**
- **How the Shorts feed tests a video, 2023.** Todd Sherman, then Shorts product lead, told Creator Insider that the feed shows a Short to a seed audience and judges it by the response. A Short may get a few hundred or a few thousand views and then drop while the system looks for its audience. Exact thresholds are not shared, to avoid gaming. He called viewed versus swiped away a useful metric; YouTube has not said that this number is the ranking signal. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/08/25/youtube-demystifies-the-shorts-algorithm-views-and-answers-other-creator-questions/), 25 Aug 2023; [Search Engine Journal](https://searchenginejournal.com/youtube-explains-how-shorts-algorithm-works/494953), 2023. **[A-press]**, old.
- **Hashtags and timing, 2023.** Sherman: hashtags are not required but can help for real-world events or specific topics; apart from breaking news, posting time should not be a concern. — [MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/388588/youtube-shorts-product-lead-offers-insights-for-cr.html), 2023. **[A-press]**, old.
- **Three-minute limit, 15 October 2024.** Announced 3 October 2024. "Starting on 15 October 2024, any uploads with a square or vertical aspect ratio that are up to three minutes in length will be considered Shorts." Three-minute Shorts could be uploaded through Studio or the mobile app; the Shorts camera could not record them at launch. The same blog post introduced a "Show fewer Shorts" control. — [YouTube Blog](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/tall-updates-coming-to-shorts/), 3 Oct 2024 **[A]**; [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15424877?hl=en) **[A]**

### Inferences

- "Best performing" has an official definition only in relative terms. On Instagram it means high average watch time, likes per reach and sends per reach, and a low skip rate. On YouTube it means a high viewed-versus-swiped-away share and a high ratio of engaged views to views. On TikTok the platform names the inputs but not their order.
- The hook is the one part of a video that two platforms measure for the creator directly: Instagram's skip rate covers the first three seconds, and YouTube's viewed versus swiped away covers the opening decision.
- A public view now means "playback started" on all three platforms. Raw view counts say nothing about retention, and Shorts view counts from before 31 March 2025 are not comparable with later ones.
- Per-reach rates, not totals, are what Instagram tells creators to watch. A small account can score well on them, because the denominator is the number of people who saw the video.
- Lengths between 61 and 180 seconds are the only range that is at once recommendable to non-followers on Instagram, classed as a Short on YouTube and eligible for TikTok Creator Rewards. This holds only if the third-party reading of TikTok's one-minute rule is right.

## 2. Large-sample studies from 2025–2026: reach, engagement, length, captions, posting frequency, format

### Takeaway

The large studies disagree on basics, including which platform gives the most views, because each draws on one tool's customers, reports means that a few viral videos inflate, and spans periods in which view definitions changed. The most defensible findings are within-account or median-based: Buffer's 2.7 million Reels show reach changing little with length below 90 seconds, slightly higher from 90 seconds to 3 minutes, and about halved above 3 minutes; Buffer's TikTok data show videos over 60 seconds with higher median reach and a typical post at roughly 500 views. No 2025–2026 large-sample study isolating the effect of captions was found.

### Cited Findings

#### Metricool, 2026 Short-Form Video Report **[B-vendor]**

- **Sample.** 6,177,375 videos published by 375,462 accounts worldwide on Instagram, TikTok and YouTube. — [Metricool](https://metricool.com/short-video-report/), 2026; publication date and data window not shown in results.
- **Views per video.** TikTok averages about 32,900 views per video, 42.6% more than the average Reel and more than six times the average Short (518.3% more). Another Metricool page, not identified in the summary, words it as "more than 32,000" views and "about 46%" more than Reels. — [Metricool repurposing guide](https://metricool.com/how-to-repurpose-video-content/), [Marketing4eCommerce](https://marketing4ecommerce.net/en/tiktok-instagram-or-youtube-which-platform-is-best-for-your-short-videos-study/), 2026.
- **By account size.** TikTok has the highest average views for tiny (under 2,000 followers), small, medium and big accounts; Instagram leads for "huge" accounts above 1 million followers. For tiny accounts TikTok nearly doubles Shorts and beats Instagram. Above 100,000 followers TikTok averages nearly 93,000 views per video, about 2.6 times Shorts. A trade headline puts small accounts at "nearly 2,500 views per video" on TikTok. — Metricool pages returned by search, among them the [benchmarks page](https://metricool.com/short-video-social-media-engagement-benchmarks-2026/) and the [state of short-form page](https://metricool.com/the-state-of-short-form-social-media/); [Metricool Italy](https://metricool.com/it/video-brevi-tiktok-instagram-youtube/); [Merca2.0](https://www.merca20.com/tiktok-is-the-best-platform-for-starting-from-scratch-small-accounts-get-nearly-2500-views-per-video/), 2026.
- **Engagement.** Reels lead in shares at every account size and also in comments. — [Metricool](https://metricool.com/short-video-report/), 2026.
- **Weaknesses.** Only accounts connected to Metricool, so self-selected toward marketers and small businesses. Arithmetic means, which viral outliers inflate. The full method, the data window and the per-platform counts sit behind a download form. Secondary figures conflict (42.6% versus 46%). "Views" are defined differently per platform and changed during 2025.
- **Related Metricool releases, headlines only.** The 2025 edition reported a "70% surge in short-form video" ([press release via Social Media Today](https://socialmediatoday.com/press-release/20250908-metricool-2025-study-unveils-70-surge-in-short-form-video-as-tiktok-conti), 8 Sep 2025). The 2026 Social Media Study covers 1,059,949 accounts and 39,762,999 posts on ten platforms and is headlined "Instagram Reels decline, YouTube views increase" ([press release](https://metricool.com/press-release-2026-social-media-study/), 9 Dec 2025). The 2026 YouTube Study covers 799,718 videos from 71,177 accounts, February 2025 against February 2026, and is headlined "more views, less watch time, and longer content shelf-life" ([press release](https://metricool.com/press-release-youtube-study-2026/), 27 Jul 2026).

#### Socialinsider, "TikTok vs. Reels vs. Shorts: 2026 Engagement Data" **[B-vendor]**

- **Sample.** Brand accounts with 1,000 to 1 million followers, active between January 2025 and June 2026, with per-platform video counts on the page (not visible in results). ### Inferences

- A mean of 32,900 views per TikTok (Metricool) and a median near 500 (Buffer) describe the same platform. The distribution is so skewed that a mean says little about a typical post. A benchmark for a small account should be a median or the account's own baseline, the way Buffer's Reels study is built.
- Socialinsider's three TikTok engagement figures can be reconciled as different periods: about 2.5% for 2024 (implied by "3.70%, up 49%"), 3.70% for 2025 and 2.60% for the first half of 2026. The denominator is still unconfirmed, so the reconciliation is tentative.
- The only large length effect in any 2025–2026 study is the 49% reach drop for Reels above 3 minutes, which matches Instagram's stated rule. Everything below that ceiling moves reach by single digits, so length should follow the content.
- Posting more often raises the odds of an outlier without raising the typical post's views (Buffer's flat median). For a small business, frequency buys more attempts, not better attempts.
- Metricool's 2026 YouTube headline ("more views, less watch time") is what the March 2025 view-count change would produce by itself; it need not mean viewers watch less.

No engagement rates, sample or method could be extracted.
- No Hootsuite benchmark data were retrieved; the search never ran.
- Socialinsider's per-platform video counts, bracket tables, shares and saves could not be read; the follow-up search did not run.
- Metricool's per-tier view numbers, data window and per-platform counts are behind a form.
- Socialinsider is also cited for a 6-million-Reel analysis that puts the best length at 45–60 seconds ([Socialinsider](https://socialinsider.io/blog/instagram-reels-length/)); its date and method were not visible.

- No controlled experiment on length, captions or frequency was found. Every study above is observational.

The data that exist compare post types, and they show carousels matching or beating Reels for brands on Instagram.

### Inferences

- For a talking-head creator the supportable position is that format has not been shown to matter independently of the signals the platforms name. The platform metrics (skip rate, watch time, sends per reach, viewed versus swiped away) are the test, and a format should be judged on an account's own numbers.
- Carousels matching Reels on views for brands means Instagram reach no longer requires video. A talking-head script can be reissued as a carousel at low cost.
- Because all three platforms now accept up to three minutes and TikTok pays only past one, the 2021–2023 advice to keep everything under 30 seconds no longer matches the incentives. That does not make long better: Buffer's data show small gains at best.

- How motion graphics, B-roll inserts or cut frequency affect retention: nothing sourced was found.

## 4. Benchmarks for a strong video: retention, completion, three-second hold, watch time, share and save rates

### Takeaway

The platforms supply the metrics but publish no "good" thresholds, and no large-sample retention-curve benchmark for 2025–2026 was found. The usable numbers are few: a typical brand Reel is watched for about four seconds, a typical TikTok post gets about 500 views, TikTok users finish 30–50% of the videos they are shown, and (on 2023 data) a Short needs at least 60% viewed versus swiped away, with strong ones at 70–90%.

### Cited Findings

- **Instagram offers a first-three-seconds metric and a retention chart, without targets.** View rate (January 2025) became skip rate (c. August 2025); a lower skip rate is better. One secondary explainer reads a high skip rate together with a low share rate as a weak hook or an unclear topic. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/01/29/instagram-gives-creators-more-insight-into-their-reels-performance), 29 Jan 2025 **[A-press]**; explainers returned by search, among them [Fanpage Karma](https://www.fanpagekarma.com/insights/instagram-insights-update/) and [Metricool](https://metricool.com/instagram-reel-analytics/) **[C]**
- **Instagram, watch time.** Users watch a Reel for an average of four seconds before moving on. — [Dash Social 2026 series](https://www.dashsocial.com/social-media-benchmarks/all-industries), 2026. **[B-vendor]**, brand accounts; the specific report was not identified.
- **TikTok, completion.** Most users in a data-donation study watch 30–50% of all videos to the end. — [arXiv 2301.04945](https://arxiv.org/html/2301.04945v2), 2023. **[B]**, old; viewer-level, not video-level.
- **TikTok, retention.** Retention averaged 23% on TikTok for fashion brands. — [Dash Social](https://www.dashsocial.com/social-media-benchmarks/fashion-industry), 2026. **[B-vendor]**; the definition of retention was not visible.
- **TikTok, watch time by length.** Videos of 5–10 seconds: median watch time 3.1 seconds, median reach 194. Videos over 60 seconds: 63.8% more watch time and 43.2% more reach than those of 30–60 seconds. — [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/longer-tiktoks-get-more-views-data/), Mar 2025. **[B]**
- **TikTok, typical views.** Median views per post are about 500 at every posting frequency. — [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/how-often-should-you-post-on-tiktok/), 2025–2026. **[B]**
- **YouTube Shorts, viewed versus swiped away.** Under 60% rarely performed well; 70–90% marked the strong performers. — [Paddy Galloway thread](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1646898356419981315.html), [Digital Information World](https://www.digitalinformationworld.com/2023/04/paddy-galloway-decoded-youtube-shorts.html), Apr 2023. **[C]**, old; 33 channels.
- **YouTube publishes no thresholds**, to avoid gaming. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/08/25/youtube-demystifies-the-shorts-algorithm-views-and-answers-other-creator-questions/), 25 Aug 2023. **[A-press]**
- **Engagement rates.** Socialinsider (brands, 2026 figure): TikTok 2.60%, Reels 0.45%, Shorts 0.30%. Dash Social (brands, January–June 2026, view-based): TikTok 3.9%, Instagram 2.6%. — [Socialinsider](https://www.socialinsider.io/blog/tiktok-vs-reels-vs-shorts/), [Dash Social](https://www.dashsocial.com/social-media-benchmarks/tiktok), 2026. **[B-vendor]**; the two are not comparable with each other.

- **Mean views per video.** TikTok about 32,900; Reels about 23,000 and Shorts about 5,300 (the last two derived by the researcher from Metricool's "42.6% more" and "518.3% more"). — [Metricool](https://metricool.com/how-to-repurpose-video-content/), 2026. **[B-vendor]**

### Inferences

- A four-second average on brand Reels means most of the audience is lost before a talking-head video finishes its first sentence. The opening line and the opening frame decide the result, which is consistent with Instagram choosing to expose a three-second metric.
- Because thresholds are unpublished, the defensible benchmark for a small account is its own trailing median for each metric, plus the trial-reel audience as a non-follower control on Instagram.
- For Shorts, the ratio of engaged views to views is a retention proxy that became available with the March 2025 change. No source gives a target for it.
- The percentages creators quote (for example a 70% completion rate or a 65% three-second hold) did not appear with a method in any source the search returned. They should be treated as folklore until sourced.

### Gaps

- No large-sample retention curves by platform, length or format were found.
- No sourced benchmark for three-second hold on organic TikTok or Shorts, for completion rate by length, or for save rate on any platform.
- Share and save rates per view: Metricool and Socialinsider appear to report them, but the figures were not visible.
- Background knowledge, unverified: paid-media practitioners use "hook rate" (three-second plays divided by impressions) with 25–30% as a common target. It applies to ads, not organic posts, and was not sourced this session.
- The search for TikTok completion and average-watch-time benchmarks did not run.

## 5. Cross-posting the same video to all three platforms; watermark and originality penalties; rules on labelling AI content

### Takeaway

No study that follows the same video across all three platforms was found; the "one video, three platforms" material compares platform averages. On the rules: Instagram does not recommend Reels carrying another app's logo (your own logo and third-party editing apps are fine) and from April 2026 removes habitual reposters from recommendations; TikTok excludes unoriginal content from the For You feed. All three require a label on realistic AI-generated video, and none requires one for ordinary editing, captions or motion graphics.

### Cited Findings

#### Cross-posting

- **Platform averages differ.** Metricool's guide says the same short video produces different results on each network and gives its averages (TikTok about 33,000 views, 42.6% more than the average Reel and more than six times the average Short). The comparison is of platform averages, not matched videos. — [Metricool](https://metricool.com/how-to-repurpose-video-content/), 2026. **[B-vendor]**
- **Practical advice from the same guide.** Keep the file under three minutes so one export fits all three; write a separate caption for each platform; stagger posting if the best times differ. — [Metricool](https://metricool.com/how-to-repurpose-video-content/), 2026. **[C]**

#### Watermarks and originality

- **Instagram, the standing rule (February 2021).** Reels "visibly recycled from other apps," meaning they contain logos or watermarks, are made less discoverable in places like the Reels tab. Followers still see them. — [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2021/02/10/instagram-reels-with-tiktok-watermark-less-discoverable/amp/), 10 Feb 2021. **[A-press]**, old but still the basis cited.
- **Instagram, your own logo is fine.** Mosseri: "What we try to do is not recommend Reels with logos from other apps, but if it's your own logo, don't worry about it." — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-clarifies-that-including-your-own-logo-on-a-reel-is-ok/730852/), date not shown (the outlet's numbering suggests late 2024). **[A-press]**
- **Instagram, third-party editors are fine.** Mosseri is reported as confirming that editing in CapCut, Templify or other apps is not a problem. — [OnlineMarketing.de](https://onlinemarketing.de/social-media-marketing/instagram-reels-watermarks-vermeiden). **[A-press]**, single German-language report.
- **Instagram, account-level penalty (30 April 2026).** Accounts that mostly post others' work lose recommendation eligibility. Press coverage adds that re-uploading someone else's photo with a different caption, border, watermark or subtitles is flagged as unoriginal. — [Instagram Creators](https://creators.instagram.com/blog/rewarding-original-creators-on-instagram), 30 Apr 2026 **[A]**; [eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/instagram-s-algorithm-clamps-down-on-repurposed--unoriginal-photos-posts) and [Digital Camera World](https://www.digitalcameraworld.com/tech/social-media/two-years-later-instagram-is-finally-giving-photographers-the-same-protection-as-videographers-with-this-key-change), 2026 **[A-press]**. The summary did not date the press detail; the context points to the 2026 change.
- **TikTok.** Unoriginal or reproduced content that has no new or creative changes is ineligible for the For You feed. No rule in the results ties a watermark as such to For You eligibility. — [TikTok Community Guidelines](https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/integrity-authenticity/), released 17 Apr 2024, revised effective 13 Sep 2025. **[A]**

#### AI labelling

- **YouTube.** Creators must disclose realistic altered or synthetic content through the "Altered content" setting at upload. Disclosure is not needed for clearly unrealistic content or for minor edits. YouTube may add a label itself, "especially if the altered or synthetic content has the potential to confuse or mislead people." Disclosing "won't limit a video's audience or impact its eligibility to earn money." — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491), current page; [YouTube Blog](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/disclosing-ai-generated-content), 18 Mar 2024. **[A]**
- **YouTube, 2026.** YouTube is reported to detect and label AI videos automatically even when the creator does not disclose, effective May 2026 by one account. — [Music Business Worldwide](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/youtube-will-now-automatically-detect-and-label-ai-videos-even-when-creators-dont-disclose-it/), 2026. **[A-press]**; not checked against YouTube's pages.
- **TikTok.** Creators must label realistic AI-generated content; unlabelled realistic clips can be removed. The label can be TikTok's own or "a clear caption, watermark, or sticker of your own." TikTok reads C2PA Content Credentials and attaches them to AI content made on the platform. In November 2025 it began adding an invisible watermark to content made with its AI tools or uploaded with C2PA credentials, and gave viewers a slider in Manage Topics to see less AI-generated content (it cannot be set to zero). TikTok says it has labelled more than 1.3 billion videos. — [TikTok Newsroom](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/more-ways-to-spot-shape-and-understand-ai-content?lang=en), 19 Nov 2025 **[A]**; [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/11/18/tiktok-now-lets-you-choose-how-much-ai-generated-content-you-want-to-see/), 18 Nov 2025 **[A-press]**
- **Instagram and Meta, post level.** Meta requires a label on realistic-looking AI or altered video and realistic-sounding AI audio, and warns "there may be penalties if you do not label content as required." If a creator does not label, a "Made with AI" label is applied when Meta detects that the content was made by AI. — [Instagram Help](https://help.instagram.com/761121959519495), undated. **[A]**
- **Instagram, automatic tags and exemptions (2026 guides).** The "AI info" tag appears when Meta's systems detect markers such as C2PA metadata or Google's SynthID watermark. AI-written captions and scripts, and AI-generated or AI-edited still images, do not require self-disclosure under the current rules. — 2026 write-ups returned by search, among them [WeRSM](https://wersm.com/instagram-ai-disclosure-rules-brands-creators/) and [WorkOS](https://workos.com/blog/instagram-ai-generated-profile-label); the summary did not say which page carries each statement. **[C]**
- **Instagram, profile level (31 August 2026).** The "AI creator" label became "AI-generated profile." Accounts whose featured person is fully synthetic and that do not carry the label can have their reach reduced; Instagram notifies them and they can add the label or appeal. The requirement "does not apply simply because a creator uses AI during the content creation process." — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/31/instagram-puts-new-limits-on-undisclosed-ai-profiles/), 31 Aug 2026; [Engadget](https://engadget.com/2246914/instagram-will-demote-ai-generated-influencers-if-they-dont-clearly-label-their-account/), 2026. **[A-press]**

### Inferences

- A filmed talking head with captions and motion graphics needs no AI label on any of the three platforms, on the rules as found. A label becomes necessary when a realistic synthetic element is added: a cloned or generated voice, a generated person, or generated footage that could pass as real. Each platform's current examples should be checked before relying on this.
- A brand logo or handle burned into the frame is safe on Instagram by Mosseri's statement. The risk is another platform's logo, so each platform should receive a clean master export, not a download from another app.
- A recurring branded template (intro card, lower third, end card) on a creator's own footage is not what the April 2026 rule targets: the rule concerns whose work the content is and treats cosmetic changes to someone else's work as unoriginal.
- TikTok's viewer slider means content labelled as AI-generated can lose distribution among viewers who turn it down. That is a reason to avoid unnecessary synthetic elements, not a reason to skip a required label.

### Gaps

- No matched-sample study (same asset, three platforms) was found.
- YouTube's position on watermarks from other apps was not retrieved; the search did not run.
- No 2025–2026 statement from any platform says a re-upload of your own video is penalized for having appeared elsewhere first, and none says it is safe.
- A marketing blog says TikTok's advertising policy has required disclosure labels on all AI-generated ad content since 21 July 2026 ([Common Thread Collective](https://commonthreadco.com/blogs/coachs-corner/tiktok-ai-ad-disclosure-rules-ecommerce-2026)); no TikTok source confirmed it.
- Legal labelling duties outside platform policy, such as the transparency obligations in the EU's AI Act, were not researched.
- Whether Instagram gives any ranking advantage to videos made in its own Edits app was not researched.

## 6. Small accounts versus large ones

### Takeaway

Instagram has said in writing that its ranking favoured large accounts and aggregators and that it changed this in April 2024; trial reels (December 2024) give any eligible account a non-follower test audience. The studies disagree on where a small account gets the most views (Metricool says TikTok, Socialinsider says Shorts), and the one median available puts a typical TikTok post at about 500 views.

### Cited Findings

- **Instagram admitted the bias and changed ranking (30 April 2024).** "Creators with large followings and aggregators of reposted content have gotten more reach in recommendations than smaller, original content creators"; a new ranking input gives smaller creators more distribution. — [Instagram Creators](https://creators.instagram.com/recommendations-and-originality), 30 Apr 2024. **[A]**
- **Three quarters of US recommendations now come from original posts.** — [Instagram Creators](https://creators.instagram.com/blog/rewarding-original-creators-on-instagram), 30 Apr 2026. **[A]**
- **Trial reels show a video to non-followers only**, with wider access since July 2025. — [Meta Newsroom](https://about.fb.com/news/2024/12/trial-reels-try-content-non-followers-first/), Dec 2024 **[A]**; [BetaNews](https://betanews.com/2025/07/14/instagram-opens-up-trial-reels-feature-to-more-creators/), 14 Jul 2025 **[A-press]**
- **Rates, not totals.** Mosseri: the rate of sends per reach matters more than the raw count. — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-chief-says-post-share-rates-are-now-a-key-driver-of-reach/716540/), 2024. **[A-press]**
- **TikTok says follower count is not a direct ranking factor**, as relayed by Sprout Social. — [Sprout Social](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/tiktok-algorithm/). **[C]** relaying an official statement.
- **YouTube tests every Short on a seed audience** and expands by response. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/08/25/youtube-demystifies-the-shorts-algorithm-views-and-answers-other-creator-questions/), 25 Aug 2023. **[A-press]**, old.
- **Metricool: TikTok is the best start for tiny accounts.** Under 2,000 followers, TikTok nearly doubles Shorts in views per video and beats Instagram; small accounts get "nearly 2,500 views per video" on TikTok. Above 100,000 followers TikTok averages nearly 93,000 views per video. Instagram leads only above 1 million followers. — [Metricool benchmarks](https://metricool.com/short-video-social-media-engagement-benchmarks-2026/), [Metricool Italy](https://metricool.com/it/video-brevi-tiktok-instagram-youtube/), [Merca2.0](https://www.merca20.com/tiktok-is-the-best-platform-for-starting-from-scratch-small-accounts-get-nearly-2500-views-per-video/), 2026. **[B-vendor]**
- **Socialinsider: Shorts lead in every follower bracket.** Secondary figures for accounts under 5,000 followers: 15,160 views per Short, 625 per Reel, 350 per TikTok. — [Socialinsider](https://www.socialinsider.io/blog/tiktok-vs-reels-vs-shorts/), 2026 **[B-vendor]**; [Revid](https://www.revid.ai/blog/short-form-video-trends) **[U]**
- **Buffer: the median TikTok post gets about 500 views** whatever the posting frequency. — [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/how-often-should-you-post-on-tiktok/), 2025–2026. **[B]**
- **Dash Social: non-followers now drive more views than followers** for brand accounts. — [Dash Social release](https://www.aap.com.au/aapreleases/globenewswire9708696/), 2026. **[B-vendor]**, headline only.
- **TikTok monetization starts at 10,000 followers** and 100,000 views in 30 days. — [TikTok Newsroom](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/introducing-the-new-creator-rewards-program), 18 Mar 2024. **[A]**

### Inferences

- All three platforms state or imply that a video is first tested on a sample and expanded by response, so follower count is not a formal gate anywhere. What a small account lacks is a warm audience to supply early likes; on Instagram the non-follower signal Mosseri names (sends per reach) does not depend on followers.
- The Metricool and Socialinsider disagreement is probably one of sample and arithmetic. Metricool's accounts are tool users including small businesses and creators, and Socialinsider's are brands. A Short keeps collecting views for months and has counted every playback start since March 2025, which lifts lifetime means. Neither figure should be offered to a small account as an expectation.
- The realistic baseline for a new or small account is hundreds of views per video, not thousands. The five-figure means are driven by outliers.

### Gaps

- No official 2025–2026 YouTube statement on small channels and Shorts reach was retrieved.
- No study reports medians by follower tier for all three platforms.
- Trial-reel eligibility thresholds differ between sources and have changed; Instagram's current help page was not seen.
- Whether the April 2024 Instagram ranking change measurably raised small accounts' reach: no independent before-and-after study was found.

## 7. What is contested, unproven, or changed recently enough that older advice is wrong

### Takeaway

Four things changed enough to invalidate older advice: the three-minute ceilings (YouTube, October 2024; Instagram, January 2025), the meaning of a view (Instagram, August 2024; YouTube, 31 March 2025), account-level originality enforcement on Instagram (April 2026), and TikTok's US algorithm retraining (January 2026). Signal weights, ideal length, the value of captions and the best platform for small accounts remain contested or unproven.

### Cited Findings

#### Changed: older advice is now wrong

- **Old advice: keep Reels under 90 seconds.** Wrong since January 2025: the limit is 3 minutes, and Instagram's 2024 slide saying longer than 90 seconds can hurt distribution is superseded. The cliff is now at 3 minutes. — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-officially-expands-reels-length-to-3-minutes/737766/), Jan 2025 **[A-press]**; [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/instagram-reels-length), 2026 **[B]**
- **Old advice: a Short is 60 seconds or less.** Wrong since 15 October 2024. — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15424877?hl=en). **[A]**
- **Old assumption: a Shorts view means someone watched.** Wrong since 31 March 2025; a view is a playback start or replay. Benchmarks from before that date need engaged views to be comparable. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/26/youtube-is-changing-how-youtube-shorts-views-are-counted), 26 Mar 2025. **[A-press]**
- **Old advice: track plays and impressions on Instagram.** Both were replaced by views in August 2024, and in the API in April 2025. — [Meta for Creators](https://creators.facebook.com/introducing-views-to-simplify-content), Aug 2024. **[A]**
- **Old advice: check your view rate.** Instagram's view rate (January 2025) was replaced by skip rate around August 2025. — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-adds-retention-insights-reels/758464/), 2025. **[A-press]**
- **Old tactic: grow an Instagram account by reposting.** Aggregators have been removed from recommendations for Reels since 2024, and for photos and carousels since 30 April 2026. — [Instagram Creators](https://creators.instagram.com/blog/rewarding-original-creators-on-instagram), 30 Apr 2026. **[A]**
- **TikTok's list of content ineligible for For You** was rewritten effective 13 September 2025. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/08/15/tiktoks-new-guidelines-add-subtle-changes-for-live-creators-ai-content-and-more/), 15 Aug 2025. **[A-press]**
- **TikTok performance data from before 2026 may not transfer to the US feed.** The US model was retrained after 22 January 2026. A single outlet, not named in the search summary, reported outages and videos stuck at zero views during the transition. No measured analysis of the effect on reach was found. — coverage returned by search, including [Forrester](https://www.forrester.com/blogs/tiktok-seals-the-deal-with-new-us-joint-venture), [BizCommunity](https://www.bizcommunity.com/article/tiktoks-new-owner-puts-apps-algorithm-in-the-spotlight-739022a) and [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/computing/tiktok/the-deal-to-keep-tiktok-in-the-us-is-now-finalized-here-are-5-things-you-need-to-know), 2026. **[C]**, forecasts and anecdote.
- **AI disclosure is no longer only self-declared.** Platforms detect and label, and Instagram attaches a reach penalty to unlabelled AI-generated profiles. — [Music Business Worldwide](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/youtube-will-now-automatically-detect-and-label-ai-videos-even-when-creators-dont-disclose-it/), 2026; [TikTok Newsroom](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/more-ways-to-spot-shape-and-understand-ai-content?lang=en), 19 Nov 2025; [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/31/instagram-puts-new-limits-on-undisclosed-ai-profiles/), 31 Aug 2026. **[A]** and **[A-press]**

#### Contested

- **Which platform gives the most views.** Metricool: TikTok, with Shorts last. Socialinsider: Shorts in every bracket. Dash Social: carousels above Reels and TikTok for brands. — [Metricool](https://metricool.com/short-video-report/), [Socialinsider](https://www.socialinsider.io/blog/tiktok-vs-reels-vs-shorts/), [Dash Social release](https://www.aap.com.au/aapreleases/globenewswire9708696/), 2026. **[B-vendor]** against **[B-vendor]**
- **Ideal length.** Reels: 90 seconds to 3 minutes for reach (Buffer, 2026), 45–60 seconds (Socialinsider), 7–30 seconds (Renderforest). TikTok: over 60 seconds (Buffer, 2025), 30–45 seconds (OpusClip, 2026). Shorts: longer performed better (Galloway, 2023). Buffer itself says length is unlikely to be decisive. — [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/instagram-reels-length), [Socialinsider](https://socialinsider.io/blog/instagram-reels-length/), [Renderforest](https://www.renderforest.com/blog/how-long-should-instagram-reel-be), [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/longer-tiktoks-get-more-views-data/), [OpusClip](https://www.opus.pro/blog/anatomy-of-a-viral-tiktok-2026), [Galloway thread](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1646898356419981315.html).
- **TikTok's engagement rate.** 3.70% and rising (Socialinsider's annual report on 2025 data), 2.60% and falling (Socialinsider's 2026 figure), 4.20% "by views" (secondary), 3.9% view-based (Dash Social). The denominators differ and are not always stated. — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/2026-social-media-benchmarks-infographic/811179/), [Socialinsider](https://www.socialinsider.io/blog/tiktok-vs-reels-vs-shorts/), [Dash Social](https://www.dashsocial.com/social-media-benchmarks/tiktok), 2026.
- **Whether Instagram counts replays as views.** Meta's page says the separate replays metric was removed and calculation is unchanged; trade coverage says repeat views are counted; one tool's summary says replays are not counted. — [Meta for Creators](https://creators.facebook.com/introducing-views-to-simplify-content), [Social Media Today](https://socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-updates-metrics-to-focus-creators-on-views/723645/), [Brandwatch help](https://social-media-management-help.brandwatch.com/en/articles/12767947).
- **The watch-time threshold for a TikTok qualified view:** 5 seconds or 30 seconds, depending on the guide. — [Metricool](https://metricool.com/tiktok-creator-next/), [Manychat](https://manychat.com/blog/tiktok-creator-rewards-program/).

#### Unproven

- **Claim: a send is worth three to five times a like.** The search summary reports the claim in some of the 2026 third-party guides it returned (the set includes [Creator Lane](https://creatorlanehq.com/blog/instagram-sends-per-reach-2026) and [BLCK Alpaca](https://blckalpaca.at/en/knowledge-base/social-media/social-media-algorithms-distribution/instagram-algorithm-2026)), one sourcing it to a Q&A. No primary source exists in the results; Mosseri's wording is "slightly more important." **[U]**
- **Claim: completion and rewatch are TikTok's top signals.** Plausible and widely repeated; the official pages found do not rank signals. — [Hootsuite](https://blog.hootsuite.com/tiktok-algorithm/). **[C]**
- **Claim: 85% of video is watched on mute.** Neither this figure nor the caption-lift percentages have a traceable study. — [DEV post](https://dev.to/clamper_ai/how-ai-caption-styles-affect-watch-time-and-engagement-409i). **[D]**
- **Claim: viewed versus swiped away is the Shorts ranking signal.** YouTube calls it a useful metric and has not said that it is the signal. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/08/25/youtube-demystifies-the-shorts-algorithm-views-and-answers-other-creator-questions/), 2023. **[A-press]**
- **Claim: Instagram's originality rule is a fixed 30-day rule.** A newsletter titled "IG's new 30-day rule will kill 50% of accounts" describes a 30-day window. No source gives a post-count threshold; reporting supports only a rolling assessment over about a month. — [Post Planner newsletter](https://milled.com/postplanner/igs-new-30-day-rule-will-kill-50-of-accounts-ejL99Dh0UIX5mqVE), 19 May 2026. **[U]**
- **Claim: TikTok and YouTube penalize watermarks.** Only Instagram has a documented statement, from 2021. — [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2021/02/10/instagram-reels-with-tiktok-watermark-less-discoverable/amp/), 10 Feb 2021. **[A-press]**

### Inferences

- Any advice or benchmark dated before 2025 needs a check against four dates: 15 October 2024, January 2025, 31 March 2025 and 30 April 2026.
- The disagreements between studies are large enough that, for an individual account, the platform-supplied metrics on its own videos outrank any published benchmark.
- Where a platform states a rule (the three-minute recommendation ceiling, originality, AI labels) the large-sample data agree with it. Where the platform is silent (length below the ceiling, captions, format) the data are weak or conflicting. Editing rules should treat the first group as constraints and the second as hypotheses to test.
